## 1. What the owner asked

The owner, 2026-10-21, as ADR-0062 quotes: "I think that apps like
android one should have a way to define bootstrap adresses". And,
2026-10-22, asked where a scan comes from: on the phone the app's own
camera, the camera permission asked the first time the person scans;
on the desktop an image file or a pasted image, no webcam.

Read here as: a person can give the app the address of someone they
want to reach, and can show their own so that someone can add them;
two phones in one room connect with nothing but the two apps.

## 2. The person's goals

- **First contact:** "Connect my phone to my friend's, here, now." Two
  people, side by side, neither knowing what an address is. They need
  to be told that both must do it, and in which order nothing matters.
- **An address sent to me:** "My colleague sent me a line of text;
  make it work." Pasted from a message, perhaps with a stray space or
  line break.
- **Being reachable:** "Show my address so they can add me." Aware or
  not that the screen discloses where the device is on the network.
- **Housekeeping:** "Why can't I reach this peer?" — which addresses
  the app knows, which are waiting for trust, which came from
  configuration.

## 3. Rules every screen here keeps

From the records, listed so a reviewer can check each screen:

- **An address grants nothing** (ADR-0062 d. 3). Adding one tells the
  device where a peer may be, never whom to trust; trust is a second,
  separate act on the same screen, and the screen says so.
- **Nothing shorter than show, scan and trust, each way** (d. 5): two
  people connect only after each has added the other's address and
  trusted the PeerId it carries. The add screen says why.
- **Only the grammar** (d. 1): a `multiaddr-with-peer-id`, direct or a
  circuit route, nothing looser. A string outside it, an address in a
  special-use class (loopback, link-local, unspecified, multicast and
  the like; private LAN ranges are admitted), a DNS name that arrived
  by scan, a code of more than 16 lines, and the 65th entry are each
  refused by name, storing nothing. A scan is all or nothing: one
  refused line refuses the scan.
- **Showing discloses** (d. 4): the show screen says it reveals this
  device's network addresses and PeerId to whoever sees it, is shown
  only on the person's act, never on a lock screen, in a notification
  or in Recents.
- **An entry the configuration file wrote** is listed as the
  operator's and cannot be removed here (d. 1, d. 2).
- **No address and no trusted peer, no silence** (d. 8): with the
  overlay empty and the trust set empty, the app says it can reach no
  one yet and what to do (add a peer by address, then trust it), and
  names the screen; it reads no connection or path signal.
- **A gesture-free, camera-free path for everything**: typing or
  pasting an address does all a scan does, and the QR is always
  beside its text.

## 4. Where things are

- **Onboarding's third step** (§5.1): after identity creation's C4
  (`design/2026-10-19-android-identity.md` §3.2) and after a restore,
  before Chats, while the device knows no address and no peer.
- **Peers** (`PeersTitle`), a new item in the Chats overflow menu,
  after "Trust" (look spec §5's menu): the list of known addresses,
  Add and Show (§5.2). Trust stays its own page; the two link to each
  other, because an address and trust are two decisions (ADR-0010).
- **The no-reach notice** on Chats (§5.6), whose action opens Peers.

## 5. Flows

### 5.1 Onboarding's third step: Connect

Shown once, after C4's Continue or a restore's last screen, when the
no-reach condition of §5.6 holds (the overlay empty and the trust set
empty). This changes where `design/2026-10-19-android-identity.md`'s C4
and R4 lead: their Continue opens Connect in that case and Chats
otherwise; a Continue whose service start is refused there still ends
on Chats with its locked notice first, as that note says, and Connect
then shows on the next start that reaches Chats with the condition
still holding. The identity note is to carry the change when it is next
revised.

- heading `ConnectTitle` ("Connect to someone");
- `ConnectBody`: InterWeave can reach a person only when this device
  knows their address and you trust them, so each of you shows your
  address and the other person adds it (no promise of finding a peer on
  the local network by itself: the Android profile authors no mDNS, and
  discovery's state reaches no client, ADR-0062 decision 8 as amended);
- two equal actions, stacked: **Add an address** (`AddAddress`, §5.3)
  and **Show my address** (`ShowMyAddress`, §5.4);
- **Not now** (`NotNow`, a text button), which opens Chats with the
  no-reach notice (§5.6).

Back here is Not now (the identity already exists, C4 says so). A
person who comes back to this screen from Add or Show finds one line
saying what was done, and the primary becoming **Continue**
(`Continue`) to Chats:
- `ConnectDoneTrusted` after an address was added and its peer is
  trusted (by step 2, or already);
- `ConnectDoneNeedsTrust` after an address was added and step 2 was
  Not now: the line says the peer is not trusted yet, and **Trust**
  (`TrustThisPeer`) beside it reopens step 2; when that peer's trust
  is not known (as §5.3 defines it),
  `ConnectDoneTrustUnknown` in its place,
  saying the address was added and that whether the peer is trusted
  could not be read, with the same Trust;
- `ConnectDoneShown` after Show alone, once it drew this device's
  addresses: the line says the other person can now add this address,
  and that this device still needs theirs.

When more than one happened, the line for what is still to do wins:
`ConnectDoneNeedsTrust` whenever any added PeerId is known not to be
trusted, else
`ConnectDoneTrustUnknown` whenever any added PeerId's trust is not
known, else
`ConnectDoneTrusted` whenever an address was added, else
`ConnectDoneShown` when Show drew this device's addresses. With
nothing added and nothing shown (Back from Add, a refusal, a failure,
an unknown add, or a Show that drew no address: `ShowNoAddress`,
`ShowReadFailed`, `ShowIncompatible`, `ShowNotPermitted`),
there is no line and the actions stay as they were. What happened is
held by the screen for this visit only: Connect opened again after a
restart starts with no line.
An add read back as checked (`AddedChecked`, §5.7) or answered
unchanged (`AddedUnchanged`, which Connect's adds read as
`AddedChecked`, §5.3) counts as added here. A line whose PeerId is
this device's own (§5.2, `PeerStateThisDevice`) counts as nothing
here: its PeerId is no added PeerId in the precedence above, so an add
of that line alone leaves the line as it was, since it brings the
person no nearer to anyone, and beside other lines only theirs decide
it. Connect shows only
after the list was read empty, so the address is on the device
because of this screen's adds, unless another admin client wrote the
same address meanwhile. That race is accepted, not tracked: telling
it apart would mean remembering what each add may have written,
across lost answers and retries, to spare a rare person a sentence
that is true of the device and only loose about who wrote it.

### 5.2 Peers (list)

App bar: back arrow, title "Peers". Top to bottom:

- **Show my address** (`ShowMyAddress`), a tonal button;
- **Add an address** (`AddAddress`), the filled primary;
- the line `PeersNoTrust` (an address lets this device reach a peer;
  it does not trust it — trust is on each row, or on Trust);
- the addresses, one row each, newest added first, then the
  configuration's: the short PeerId as `ShortPeer` makes it, after
  the names of the identity in front's contacts on that PeerId when
  there are any (`human-client-ui.md` §3): every one, since several
  identities of one node share its PeerId (ADR-0061) and naming one
  would hide the others, in the locale's collation order (until the
  client has a collator, §11 Q-collation: by each name's Unicode
  lowercase, then by the names' code points), one name
  per line, never joined into one string, since a name is typed by
  a person and may itself hold "and" or a comma ("Mum and Dad",
  "Smith, John"), which a joined list would read as more contacts
  than there are; each name is isolated plain text, as §3 renders
  every received name, and wraps within its line rather than
  truncating; the screen reader reads each name as an item of its
  own, never one label joining them; the short PeerId always stays beside them since two
  contacts may share a name; another identity's contacts are never
  consulted or named (language-culture-en's note on REPLY 01a16654);
  under it
  the address's host and port in the code font, and one state in
  words:
  - `PeerStateTrusted` ("Trusted: this device tries to connect to this
    address": the client knows the address is dialled, never that the
    peer is reachable),
  - `PeerStateNeedsTrust` ("Not trusted: this device does not connect
    to this address"), with **Trust** (`TrustThisPeer`) on the row,
  - `PeerStateProviderOff` ("Not used: this device's configuration
    turns this list off"), when the operator's file set the provider off (d. 2);
  - `PeerStateThisDevice` (this address is this device's own), for a
    row whose PeerId is this device's (the profile PeerId the client
    learned when its session opened, `LOCAL-CLIENT.md` `local_peer`),
    in place of the trusted or not-trusted state and whatever the
    trust set says, with no **Trust**: the runtime refuses to trust
    this device's own PeerId (`LOCAL-IPC.md`, `admin.trust.set`), so
    a Trust there could only fail; a provider-off row
    still reads `PeerStateProviderOff`;
  and the origin: `PeerAddedHere` or `PeerFromConfig`.
- Each row's menu (⋮, `PeerMenu`): **Copy address** (`CopyAddress`),
  **Remove** (`RemoveAddress`) on an added row only; on a
  configuration row the menu says `PeerConfigNotRemovable` in place of
  Remove.

States: **empty** — `PeersEmpty` (no addresses yet; what an address is
for, and the two actions above); **at the bound** — with 64 entries,
Add an address shows `PeersLimit` under it and opens nothing;
**refused** (the add method off because the operator's file turns the
list off) — Add shows `PeersListOff` and opens nothing;
**unreadable** (the list read failed, §5.7) — under Show my address,
in place of Add and the rows, the read's problem line and, unless the
problem is one no retry mends, **Try again** (`TryAgain`), which reads
the list again. Add is not shown there, since neither the bound nor
the list-off state can be known; Show stays, its page having its own
failure state (§5.4); **trust unread** (the address list read, the
trust set not known: its read failed or was refused, or a Trust's
answer was lost and not read back) — the rows show no trust state,
neither `PeerStateTrusted` nor `PeerStateNeedsTrust`, since either
would be a guess; origin, host and `PeerStateProviderOff` still show,
and **Trust** stays on every row not in `PeerStateProviderOff` or
`PeerStateThisDevice`, which still shows (a
Trust on a peer already trusted changes nothing, and the trust step
reads the set itself). In place of `PeersNoTrust`, which points at the
rows' trust states, one line says why they are missing:
`PeersTrustUnread` with **Try again** (`TryAgain`), which reads the
trust set again, or, when the trust read was refused,
`PeersTrustNotPermitted` with no Try again. An incompatible trust
read has no line of its own: `admin.trust.list` is IPC 2.1, below
`admin.peers`' 2.7 (`LOCAL-IPC.md`), so a runtime that answered the
address list serves it, and no version difference can produce one;
should one come, it shows `PeersTrustUnread` with no Try again, a
retry being unable to mend it. A row whose PeerId a Trust from this page has just confirmed
shows `PeerStateTrusted`. While the address list is unreadable as
well, the unreadable state stands alone (there are no rows), and its
Try again reads both lists.

**Remove** runs at once: the entry leaves the overlay and is dialled
no more (d. 2). Snackbar `ConfirmAddressRemoved` with Undo, which adds
it again, the same string. On a row in `PeerStateProviderOff` the
snackbar has no Undo, since the add is refused while the
configuration turns the list off (d. 2), and its line says so
(`ConfirmAddressRemovedFinal`). An Undo the device refuses for another
reason leaves the address removed and shows that refusal's own
string: `PeersLimit` when the bound was reached meanwhile,
`PeersListOff` when the list was turned off meanwhile, and, for a
failure that wrote nothing (§5.7's first row), `UndoFailed` (the address was not added
back) with **Try again** (`TryAgain`), which holds the removed address
for the snackbar's window and calls the add again; its outcomes are
Undo's: success restores the row and focus goes to it, a refusal shows
its own string and a failure `UndoFailed` again. Focus otherwise goes
as after Remove. A kill inside the window
leaves it removed. Remove changes no trust: the snackbar's line says so only if
the peer is trusted (`ConfirmAddressRemovedTrusted`: removed; you
still trust this peer, and may reach it by another address); while
trust is unread it is `ConfirmAddressRemoved`.

A Remove that wrote nothing (§5.7's first row) leaves the row as it
was and shows the snackbar `RemoveFailed` (the address is still in the list) with
**Try again**, which calls the removal again; focus returns to that
row's menu button. A Remove, an Undo or a Try again whose answer was
lost is read back first (§5.7). A Remove or an Undo answered not
permitted or incompatible puts Peers in its unreadable state with
that answer's line (§5.7), focus on the line.

**Trust** on a row opens §5.3's trust step for that PeerId.

### 5.3 Add an address

A full-screen page on a narrow layout, a dialog on a wide one. Two
steps on one screen, the second appearing under the first once the
first has done its work (d. 3, d. 5).

**Step 1, the address.**

- the field `AddressField` ("Address"), multi-line, monospace,
  accepting a typed or pasted address; line breaks are kept, so a
  pasted list of lines is a list, a line ending in any form (LF, CR LF
  or CR) ending a line; before the check each line is trimmed of
  spaces and tabs at both ends, and empty lines are dropped,
  so a stray space or a trailing line break never refuses an address;
  what remains is checked and counted;
- **Scan a code** (`ScanCode`, Android) or **Open an image…**
  (`OpenCodeImage`) and **Paste an image** (`PasteCodeImage`) on the
  desktop (§5.5); Ctrl+V in the field always pastes text, never an
  image, since an image's code is added at once and a clipboard often
  holds both: an image is added only through Paste an image. A scan
  runs only while the field is empty — no line left once trimmed and
  empty lines dropped, as for Add — so a code and typed lines are never
  on the page at once: with an address in the field, Scan a code, Open
  an image… and Paste an image stay visible, and pressing one does
  nothing but show `ScanAfterField` under them (add or clear the typed
  address first), focus on that line, read after the scan controls; it
  goes when the field becomes empty or an add runs. Add with an empty
  field stores nothing and shows `AddressNeeded` under the field, focus
  on the field; it goes when the field is no longer empty or an add
  runs;
- the helper `AddressHelp`: an address starts like
  `/ip4/192.168.1.20/tcp/4001/p2p/` and ends with a PeerId; ask the
  other person to show theirs;
- **Add** (`AddAction`), filled.

On Add: each line is checked by the grammar and the special-use floor
before anything is stored; one refused line refuses all (`AddRefused`
under the field names the line and the reason, one of `RefusedGrammar`,
`RefusedSpecialUse`, `RefusedDnsFromScan`, `RefusedTooMany`,
`RefusedLimit`, `RefusedListOff`), focus on the field with the line
selected. A line whose PeerId is this device's own is not refused
until architect-cto rules on it (§11, Q-own): the proposal is a
refusal before anything is sent, `RefusedOwnAddress`, since such an
address reaches no one and its row can never be trusted; until then
it is added, and its row reads `PeerStateThisDevice` (§5.2). A scanned code never goes through the field (§5.5): it is
added as one add, all of its lines or none (d. 4), so nothing of a code
can be edited, pruned or mixed with typed lines before it is stored.

A typed or pasted add holds at most 16 lines, counted after trimming
and dropping empty lines, as a code does (d. 4) and as the add of
several lines takes in one call (§5.7, a method not yet recorded): one
add stays all or nothing. More is refused
before anything is sent, storing nothing, with `RefusedTooManyLines`
under the field, which says how many lines there are and the most one
add takes; focus on the field with line 17 to the end selected, so
the person can cut them and add them next. Nothing is split into
several adds unasked, since a split add can stop half done. Until
a build binds the add of several lines (§5.7), the bound is 1: the
same refusal with `{max}` filled as 1, focus on the field with line
2 to the end selected, so a paste of the other device's Copy all
addresses (§5.4) takes one cut and one Add (rust-ui-dev-01's
QUESTION 01a1655d and this role's REPLY 01a1655e, on #384). Until
then the add page also shows no scan control: Scan a code, Open an
image… and Paste an image are not shown, as on a phone with no camera
(§5.5), since a code may carry several lines; the reading order is
then field, helper (and its line when shown), Add (§6).

Accepted: the step collapses to one line naming what was added:
`AddedLine` when the lines added carry one PeerId,
`AddedLineSeveral` when they carry more (it counts the addresses and
the peers, and names none: each peer is named in its own block
below), `AddedUnchanged` when every line was already there. On a page
opened from Connect, `AddedChecked` takes `AddedUnchanged`'s place:
Connect opens only on a list read empty, so a line already there is
one this visit's earlier add wrote (§5.1), and "Nothing was added"
would be followed by Connect's "You added an address". A line
answered unchanged beside lines added is not counted. Step 2 appears,
with one block per PeerId of the add that is not already trusted
(several are possible in one paste or code); when the trust set could
not be read, one block per PeerId of the add, since a needless block
costs a tap and a missing one leaves a peer untrusted with no prompt.
This device's own PeerId never has a block (§5.2,
`PeerStateThisDevice`).

**Step 2, trust** (only for a PeerId not already trusted):

- `TrustStepTitle` ("Do you trust {peer}?"), the full PeerId in the
  code font with Copy PeerId, and `TrustStepBody`: InterWeave connects
  to this address only after you trust this PeerId; trust lets this
  peer exchange messages with you; before Trust, compare the PeerId
  with the one the other person's screen shows;
- `TrustStepBoth`: the other person has to add your address and trust
  you too; each device connects only to peers it trusts, so nothing
  connects until both of you have done this (d. 5);
- **Trust** (`TrustThisPeer`, filled) and **Not now** (`NotNow`), in
  each block.

Trust writes that PeerId's trust entry (as Trust's own button does,
the same confirmation rules, ADR-0012) and the block reads
`TrustStepDone`; Not now leaves the address added and its row reading
`PeerStateNeedsTrust` (with no trust state while trust is unread,
§5.2), and the block reads `TrustStepLater` when the PeerId's trust
is known, or `TrustStepLaterUnknown` when it is not, which says Trust
was not chosen and claims no trust state. A PeerId's trust is
**known** when the latest thing that told this device about it
succeeded and no Trust has been lost since: the trust set's read when
step 2 opened, a Trust answered as made (known, trusted), or this
block's read back after a lost Trust. A Trust whose answer was lost makes it **not
known** until a read back of it succeeds, whatever an earlier read
showed; a read that failed leaves it not known. Such a Trust is
**pending** until a read of the trust set started after it was sent
succeeds (any read:
this block's read back, a step 2 opening, Peers' own) or the app
process ends; the device holds it per PeerId across pages, so a
Trust made meanwhile on that PeerId in any **trust step of this
note** retries it (§5.7): step 2 however it is opened (from Add, from
a row's Trust, one opened while trust is unread included, or reopened
by Connect's Trust, §5.1). The Trust settings page is
`human-client-ui.md` §8's, outside this note (§11, Q-pending).

**Known and the end of pending are the client's best evidence, not a
guarantee**: the runtime can still apply a lost Trust after answering
a later read on another connection (p2p-network-dev-02's REPLY
01a16662, relayed by rust-ui-dev-01's INFO 01a16665). Every text that
rests on a known not-trusted state, or on pending having ended, can
therefore be overtaken by a late write: `TrustStepNotMade`,
`TrustStepLater`, `PeerStateNeedsTrust`, `ConnectDoneNeedsTrust`, and
the not-written texts a Trust shows once nothing is pending (§5.7).
Whether a contract change should bound it is asked in §11, Q-late. A block in which Trust was pressed never reads
`TrustStepLater…`: Not now or Done leaves its last line as it stands
(a problem text, `TrustStepNotMade` or `TrustStepUnknown`), each of
which is already true of it. A block decided by Not now keeps
**Trust** until the page closes, so a line that says to choose Trust
names a control that is there; Not now leaves it. Pressing that
Trust makes the block undecided, as in any undecided block: a Trust
made decides it again, and any other outcome leaves it undecided with
its line, as below. With one
block, deciding it closes the page; with several, the page closes once
every block is decided, or on **Done** (`Done`, below the blocks),
which leaves any undecided one as Not now. On closing, one snackbar:
`ConfirmPeerTrusted` when every block was trusted, else **the add's
snackbar**: `ConfirmAddressAdded` when step 1 read `AddedLine` or
`AddedLineSeveral`, else step 1's own line, `AddedUnchanged` or
`AddedChecked`, neither of which says an address was added. When no
PeerId of the add needs trust, there is no step 2 and the page closes
with the add's snackbar. Focus after a
block is decided goes to the next undecided block's title, else to
Done.

While a block's Trust awaits its answer, its Trust and Not now give
way to its state line, `TrustStepWaiting` (saying that
InterWeave is trusting {peer} and the answer is awaited, claiming no
outcome), with an indeterminate progress mark that is still under
reduced motion; the line is focusable, and the answer replaces it with
the block's next line as below. The block is neither decided nor
undecided while it waits. **Done** while any block waits, or Back or
Escape, closes the page at once: a waiting block counts as a Trust
whose answer was lost, its trust not known (as defined above), so the
snackbar is the add's and Connect's line is chosen by §5.1's
precedence with that PeerId not known; its answer, when it comes, is
shown only by what Peers reads next.

A Trust in a block that the device does not make leaves the block
undecided and shows, inside it under its buttons, the Trust page's own
text for that problem (`TrustUnavailable`, `TrustNotPermitted`,
`TrustRefused`, `TrustIncompatible`, `TrustFailed`), except where
a Trust on that PeerId is pending (§5.7) and this one writes nothing
(`TrustUnavailable` or `TrustFailed`): the block reads
`TrustStepUnknown` (§5.7, an unknown change stays unknown); focus goes
to that line. Trust and Not now stay, except that Trust leaves the block
for `TrustNotPermitted` and `TrustIncompatible`, which no retry mends.
A Trust whose answer was lost shows `TrustUnconfirmed` in the block
and reads the trust set again: trusted, the block reads
`TrustStepDone` and counts as decided; not trusted,
`TrustStepNotMade` (the change was not made; Trust again), the block
undecided; the read failing, `TrustStepUnknown`
(this device could not check whether {peer} is trusted; Trust again is
safe), the block undecided. Done leaves any undecided block as Not
now, so Connect's line is `ConnectDoneNeedsTrust`
(`ConnectDoneTrustUnknown` for a block whose trust is not known) and
the snackbar the add's (above).

Back or Escape on step 1 discards what was typed (the field asks
nothing: an address is easy to paste again); on step 2 it is Done.

### 5.4 Show my address

Everything this page shows comes from one read of this device's
addresses: `admin.peers.own_addresses`, at IPC 2.8 in
p2p-network-dev-02's #378, not yet on main. Until it lands, this whole
section, and acceptance 6, 7 and 10's Show cases, wait on it as §5.7's
add of several lines does.

A full-screen page on both layouts, opened only by the person's act:

- first, `ShowWarning` in body medium, before the code: anyone who
  sees this screen can learn this device's addresses and PeerId;
- the QR code, centred, at least 240 dp a side, dark modules on a
  white square with a four-module quiet zone in both themes (a scanner
  needs the contrast; it is not themed), at most 16 lines in the
  order d. 4 states;
- under it the same addresses as text, one per line, selectable, and
  **Copy all addresses** (`CopyAddresses`);
- the PeerId alone, with Copy PeerId, so the other person can compare
  it at the trust step.

The page is never left on screen unasked: it closes when the app goes
to the background or the screen locks, Recents shows no content for it
(`FLAG_SECURE` on this page alone on Android, as on the phrase pages),
and it is never put in a notification. Back closes it.

**No address to show** (no listen address in the admitted classes, no
confirmed external address, no circuit route): the code is not drawn;
`ShowNoAddress` says this device has no address another device can
reach yet, why in one line (`ShowNoAddressOffline` when offline,
`ShowNoAddressLocalOnly` when only special-use addresses are bound),
and what to do: connect this device to a network, or add the other
person's address instead, since this device can still dial them.

**The addresses could not be read**: never "no address", which the
read has not shown. The code is not drawn; in its place stands
`ShowIncompatible` when the runtime answers the read as unsupported (a
desktop daemon without Show's read, above),
`ShowNotPermitted` when it answers `CapabilityDenied`, both without Try
again, and `ShowReadFailed` for any other failure, with **Try again**
(`TryAgain`), which reads again. Focus on the line, after the warning.

### 5.5 Scanning a code

Until a build binds the add of several lines (§5.7), none of this
section's controls is shown (§5.3's interim state), and this section,
its states in §7 (camera denied, denied for good, image without a
code), its focus rows in §8 and the acceptance on scanning wait on it.

**Android**, Scan a code:

- the first time, the camera permission is asked by the system dialog,
  after one line in the app (`CameraWhy`: InterWeave uses the camera
  only to read an address code, and keeps no picture); the permission
  is asked on this tap and nowhere else, never at install or in the
  background (the owner, 2026-10-22; ADR-0062 decision 8 as amended on
  #362, which admits `CAMERA` to the manifest's pinned set and nothing
  else new);
- a phone with no camera installs too (the camera is not a required
  feature, the same amendment): Scan a code is not shown there, and
  typing or pasting is the way;
- granted: a full-screen viewfinder with a square guide, the line
  `ScanHint`, and a Close (✕, `CloseScanner`); a code read closes the
  viewfinder and is added at once, its lines as one add and never put
  into the field: accepted, the page goes to its result and step 2 as
  for a typed add; refused, nothing is stored, the field stays as it
  was, and `AddRefused` names the line and its reason;
- denied: back to the add page with `CameraDenied` (the camera is
  needed only to scan a code; you can type or paste the address instead), focus on
  the field; denied for good: `CameraDeniedSettings`, with **Open
  settings** (`OpenAppSettings`);
- a code that is not an address list (any other QR): `ScanNotAddress`,
  the viewfinder stays open.

**Desktop**, Open an image… opens the file dialog filtered to PNG and
JPEG; Paste an image reads an image from the clipboard. Either decodes
the first QR code in the image; none found: `ImageNoCode`; then as a
scan: added at once as one add, never put into the field.

A scanned DNS name is refused by name (`RefusedDnsFromScan`), the same
string typed is admitted (d. 1). A scan's lines never enter the field,
so a person who wants the name types it whole; a line counts as typed
only when the person typed it.

### 5.6 The no-reach notice

On Chats, while the overlay is empty (`admin.peers.list_addresses`
lists nothing) and the trust set is empty (the trust read): a notice
banner (look spec §5's notice slot) `NoReachNotice` (InterWeave cannot
reach anyone yet: add a peer by address, then trust it) with **Peers**
(`PeersTitle`), which opens §5.2. The condition is these two facts and
nothing else (ADR-0062 decision 8 as amended on #362): no connection,
path or discovery signal, which name no peer or reach no client. With
a trusted peer and no address the notice does not show, since an
authored entry or the local network may carry that peer. It goes when
either list gains an entry; it is not dismissible, since it describes a
state, not news.

Both facts must be read: a list that could not be read is unknown, not
empty, and the notice does not show on it, nor does Connect (§5.1),
since "cannot reach anyone" would then be a guess. The Peers page,
opened from the menu, says what could not be read (§5.2).

### 5.7 When a call fails or its answer is lost

The three address methods answer a refusal as an answer
(`AddressChange::Refused`, §5.3's reasons) and a failure as an error.
The person is told one of these, by what is true; which error falls in
which row is the builder's mapping, and the rule for it is the last
column:

| outcome | the person is told | it holds when |
|---|---|---|
| not written | `AddFailed` (add), `RemoveFailed`, `UndoFailed`: nothing changed, with Try again | the request never left (cancelled before dispatch, no connection made), or the contract says nothing was written (`Internal` on an add or a removal) |
| unknown | nothing yet: the list is read back first, then as below | the request left and no answer came (a timeout, the runtime gone or shutting down mid-call, a raced cancel) |
| not permitted | `PeersNotPermitted`, no Try again | `CapabilityDenied` |
| incompatible | `PeersIncompatible`, no Try again | `ProtocolUnsupported` or `VersionIncompatible`: a desktop daemon older than this app (no `admin.peers`, IPC below 2.7) |
| several not taken | `AddSeveralIncompatible` under Add, the field kept (empty after a scan), no Try again | an add of more than one line (typed, pasted or scanned) answered `ProtocolUnsupported` or `VersionIncompatible` by a daemon that has `admin.peers` but not the add of several lines (below the minor that method's record gives); a one-line add still works there |

"Nothing was changed" is said only in the first row, or after a read
back shows it.

**The read back** after an unknown answer reads the list once, and
decides by what it lists:

| the call | every line listed | not every line listed | the read fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add (typed, pasted or scanned) | accepted, with `AddedChecked` (this device has these addresses; it does not say they were added, which the read cannot show) and step 2 | `AddFailed`, the field kept | `AddUnknown`, the field kept, focus on Add: Add again is safe, since an address already stored answers unchanged |
| Remove | `RemoveFailed` | as Remove done: the snackbar with Undo | `RemoveUnknown` with Try again, removing again being safe; the row stays as last read, and the list is read again when the snackbar leaves, and that read failing puts Peers in its unreadable state (§5.2) |
| Undo or its Try again | as Undo done: the row back, focus on it | `UndoFailed` | `UndoUnknown` with Try again |

**An unknown change stays unknown until a read shows it.** A retry
of a change whose answer was lost and not read back, when the retry
writes nothing (the first table's first row), keeps the unknown line
as it was, read again to the screen reader so the retry is known to
have run, with focus as that line's own row puts it: Try again on
`RemoveUnknown` or `UndoUnknown` keeps the snackbar, Add again on the
same lines (as trimmed, §5.3) after `AddUnknown` keeps `AddUnknown`,
and Trust again in a block reading `TrustStepUnknown` brings it back
after `TrustStepWaiting`. For a Trust, "writes nothing" is
`TrustUnavailable` or `TrustFailed`; `TrustRefused`,
`TrustNotPermitted` and `TrustIncompatible` are answers and go by
them, the last two taking Trust away as §5.3 says. It never shows the
not-written text (`RemoveFailed`, `UndoFailed`, `AddFailed`, or the
Trust page's problem text such as `TrustUnavailable`), whose "still
in the list", "still removed" or "nothing was changed" is not known,
since the first write may have landed. A retry that gets an answer
goes by that answer. Anything else stands alone and says its own
outcome: an add of lines that differ from the unknown add's (the
field edited), and any add on a page opened again, since the page
holds what it sent only while it is open. A Trust is a retry of an
unknown change exactly when a Trust on that PeerId is pending (§5.3),
in any trust step of this note (as §5.3 defines it): a Trust that
writes nothing then reads `TrustStepUnknown`.
With nothing pending on that PeerId (its trust read since, or trust
unread only because a read failed or was refused), a Trust that
writes nothing shows its not-written text, true on the client's best
evidence (§5.3, known)
(rust-ui-dev-01's QUESTIONs 01a16622 and 01a16651, on #384).

An add is all or none: one line is one write, and several lines are
one call that checks every line before it writes any. **That call is
not yet in the contracts**: `LOCAL-IPC.md` has `admin.peers.add_address`
(one address, IPC 2.7) only. p2p-network-dev-02 has agreed to add it
(`admin.peers.add_addresses`, planned for IPC 2.9; rust-ui-dev-01's
REPLY 01a1647e), and its record is theirs (ADR-0017's rows). Until it
lands, every rule of this note on an add of several lines (§5.3's
16-line bound, this paragraph, the "several not taken" row above, and
acceptance 3's 16-line code and its refused-line scan, 4's scanned
cases, 6, 8's two PeerIds in one add, 9, and 10,
for a code or paste of several lines) is waiting
on it, and those acceptance cases cannot be met before it. Its interim
state is §5.3's: a typed or pasted add takes one line until the build
binds the method, and no scan control is shown before then (§5.3,
§5.5), so `AddSeveralIncompatible` is never shown by a build that never
asked. The embedded runtime is expected to gain the
method in the same build as the daemon, which makes
`AddSeveralIncompatible` a daemon's state only; if the record leaves
the embedded runtime out, this section gains its state. If the record differs from what is written here
(all or none, at most 16 lines), this section is revised to it. So a
read back that lists some of an
add's lines and not others shows an add not made, the listed lines
having been there before, and is `AddFailed`; nothing is shown as
partly added. Several lines are never sent as one call per line,
before the method or after. Should the add of several lines ever write part
of a list, this rule is reopened, not bent.

**A list that cannot be read** when Peers opens shows §5.2's
unreadable state with the read's line: `PeersReadUnavailable` (the
runtime cannot be reached), `PeersNotPermitted`, `PeersIncompatible`,
or `PeersReadFailed` for anything else; Try again for the first and
the last. Add's page reads no list: the add's own answers decide
(unchanged included), and only step 2 reads the trust set. A not
permitted or incompatible answer to a change shows its line under Add
on the add page, the field kept, and puts Peers in the unreadable state
when it is next shown; on Peers, a Remove or an Undo answered so puts
the page in that state at once (§5.2).

## 11. Questions answered

By architect-cto-01, DECISION 01a16276 (2026-10-22), in ADR-0062's
amendment on BlueTeam-OU/InterWeave#362 (fa9cb2cf):

- **Q1** — the camera: decision 8 now carries the app's own camera on
  Android, opened on the Scan screen alone, `CAMERA` asked at the first
  Scan and never at install or in the background, declared without a
  required feature; on the desktop an image file or a pasted image, no
  webcam; the manifest check's pinned set admits `CAMERA` by this
  record and nothing else new. ADR-0059 decision 6 (no contacts
  permission) is unchanged. §5.5 and acceptance 9.
- **Q2** — the notice: discovery's state reaches no client session
  (LOCAL-CLIENT.md's ServerState carries health and the connectivity
  summary only). The first answer keyed the notice on the overlay, the
  trust set and the summary; the amendment's later rounds on #362
  (8fc00448, bf4cee0e) narrowed it to exactly two facts, the overlay
  empty and the trust set empty, never a connection or path signal, and
  with a trusted peer and no address no notice. §5.6, §5.1 and
  acceptance 2 follow the narrowed rule.

Still open, for architect-cto:

- **Q-own** — an address naming this device's own PeerId: the runtime
  stores it (no refusal of `admin.peers.add_address` covers it), and
  it lets this device reach no one. This note proposes that the
  client refuse it before anything is sent, with `RefusedOwnAddress`
  under the field (§5.3), and asks whether that refusal, or one in the
  runtime, is the product's rule. Until it is ruled, such an address
  is added and its row reads `PeerStateThisDevice` (§5.2).
- **Q-pending** — a Trust whose answer was lost is pending per PeerId
  (§5.3), and a Trust on this note's surfaces that writes nothing
  meanwhile reads `TrustStepUnknown` rather than "Nothing was
  changed". The Trust settings page (`human-client-ui.md` §8) makes
  the same write and shows its own texts. Should the same rule hold
  there? This note asks; it changes nothing on that page.
- **Q-collation** — a row's contacts' names are in the locale's
  collation order; the client has no collator yet, and adding one is a
  dependency decision. Until it is made, names are ordered by each
  name's Unicode lowercase, then by their code points (§5.2). Should
  the client take a collator dependency, and until then is this order
  the rule?
- **Q-late** — a Trust whose answer was lost can land after a later
  read has answered, so "known" and the end of "pending" are the
  client's best evidence, and every text §5.3 lists under that limit
  can be overtaken by the late write. The harmful case is a lost
  allow, then a revoke answered as made, then the allow landing: the
  person is told the peer is not trusted while the runtime trusts it
  (the revoke is the Trust settings page's; on this note's surfaces
  the near case is a lost allow, a read showing not trusted, then Not
  now, where the late allow matches what the person asked).
  p2p-network-dev-02 is raising the contract change (a request token
  or a drained connection) with architect-cto; once it is decided,
  §5.3's end of pending follows it. Which contract change, if any?
