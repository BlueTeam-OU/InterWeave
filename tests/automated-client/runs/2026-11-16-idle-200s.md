## What ran

Two daemons in two network namespaces joined by the overlay's link,
only A holding a static-bootstrap entry for B, so B reaches A only over
the connection A dialled. Connected, then idle for 200 s — no client
and no channel on either host — with both ends' `admin.peers.list`
read every quarter second. Then B's client sent a direct message to
A's over that connection. Then the link was deleted, and both ends
had to log the close the idle watch looked for: the control that the
absence of a `peer disconnected` line during the idle is a reading of
a line that is written.

## What it shows, and what it does not

Both ends held the connection through 200 s idle, more than three of
the daemon's 60 s idle timeouts, with no close logged at either end
and no read of either end's peer list showing it closed; B's message
then landed at A over it; and the close on the link's loss was logged
at both ends.

As with the 75 s case, this pins the property and not the mechanism:
between two daemons the pinned gossipsub's parked stream holds the
connection as well as the data-plane hold does (`held_data_plane.rs`
is the hold's own proof, against a peer that runs no gossipsub).

Not shown, by name: two real hosts. The namespaces share one kernel,
so the path has no NAT mapping, firewall or middlebox that could expire
an idle flow, no real latency or loss, and the idle period is 200 s,
not the hours a fleet host sits idle.
