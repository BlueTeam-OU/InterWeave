## Findings

1. **The relayed `paths` case, first exchange.**
   - The phone's runtime came up over the relay. D logged `peer connected
     … path="relayed"` at 14:01:46.727Z, and D took the phone's message.
   - That connection closed at 14:01:46.898Z (`reason="closed"`). The
     phone's instrumentation finished at 18:01:46.961 phone time.
   - D's answer never found a route. D's dial back failed (`dial_failed`),
     and a `relay_circuit` retry with a 30 s backoff outlasted the
     deadline: `no route to <phone> within the deadline`.
   - The phone's case ended 0.23 s after connecting, and its outcome is
     not in this log. The harness reported the case's outcome only after
     the desktop's half returned, and here the desktop's half panicked
     first. This run was read as the case returning an error; the
     diagnostic run below showed it passing.
   - The section "the app's log" is empty, yet the app does log, under
     the tag `interweave`, its connectivity events included. That was
     seen in the phone's full logcat during the rerun. The harness's log
     read by uid (`logcat --uid`) answered nothing.
   - Both were harness defects. They were fixed on this branch before
     the diagnostic run: a desktop-side failure now prints what the
     phone's case answered (`CaseRun::so_far`), and the app's lines are
     read by the pids ActivityManager names.
   - **The diagnostic run (15:10Z, host at `6004f4b5`, the same phone
     build), the relayed case alone, failed on its first try. Its output
     is below.** The phone's case PASSED: `{"path":"relayed","result":"pass"}`.
     So it had taken D's answer and been told the route began relayed.
     D's half still reported the answer unrouted. The phone's runtime
     logged `e2e paths: stopped, runtime Ok(0)` 0.48 s after its relayed
     connection came up, as the app's e2e runner stops the service once a
     case returns. The answer crossed; its acceptance did not come back
     before the phone's runtime stopped. `TRANSPORT.md` "Outcome unknown"
     names exactly this: a request may have reached and been accepted,
     and a closed connection after it reads `PeerUnreachable`, "not
     confirmed". The transport kept its contract. The test asked for a
     confirmation that its own design, ending the phone's process as it
     read the answer, could lose. (`send_until_routed`'s "no route"
     wording is the helper's for any refusal at its deadline, not the
     transport's class.)
   - The fix, on this branch: once the desktop's answer is accepted, the
     desktop sends a closing message, and the phone's half ends only on
     it (`close_exchange`). Its own outcome is not relied on. On the
     stand-ins it passes, and each of its tests fails when its part is
     removed. **It is not yet proved on a device**: the phone's half is
     compiled into the androidTest APK, so it needs a build that carries
     it.
2. **No notification during `human_chat`.** In the `human_chat` rerun,
   the app's notification records were counted every 1.5 s from
   14:06:30Z to 14:06:55Z; the count was 0 throughout. The first run's
   count produced no lines. The instrumented case delivers to the app's
   service host inside its own process and posts no notification. Gate
   (b)'s notification line therefore needs the app in its normal mode,
   not these cases.
3. **The foreground service.** No foreground-service line for the app
   appears in the phone's logcat during the cases, and the services dump
   after the run lists none, because the cases force-stop the app. So
   this run does **not** record §20 step 3, the foreground service on API
   34+ under ADR-0041 `remoteMessaging`. That needs the app running as
   installed.

## What this run does not prove

- A radio network. The phone reaches loopback through adb's reversed
  ports.
- The desktop–Android platform matrix (j13), which is carried
  separately.
- That finding 1's fix holds on a device. That needs a phone build with
  the fixed `android-e2e-cases`.
- §20 step 3 and gate (b)'s notification line (findings 2 and 3).
