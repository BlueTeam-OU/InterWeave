# Human-client application state and persistence

Status: design only. Message-content retention is normative through [`RETENTION.md`](./RETENTION.md) and ADR-0044.

## Selected store

Use a versioned SQLite database behind a Rust `human-store` crate on desktop and Android. The application storage schema is independent of transport configuration/state and may be deleted/rebuilt without changing PeerId or trust policy.

The store is **not** a conventional permanent conversation-history database. Message content is durable only in the retention states defined by `RETENTION.md`.

The store is encrypted at rest as a whole — pages, write-ahead log and journal — under a per-profile store key the platform's custody seals (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0060): on Android an `AndroidKeyStore`-wrapped key under the `background-compatible` policy, on the v1 desktop one owner-only sealed key file `<state_dir>/human-store.key`, under the state root and outside this directory so a copy of `<state_dir>/human` leaves it behind, at ADR-0038's `filesystem-only` level, moving into ADR-0038's envelope when it lands. The mechanism is SPIKE-011's to select within ADR-0060 decision 4; an invalidated key is a store-health condition with discard-and-recreate offered directly; a missing key or a damaged file goes to recovery mode (§Migrations), never an identity condition.

## Logical tables

Conceptually:

```text
contacts(contact_id, local_endpoint_id, display_name, name_key, shape_key, compat_shape_key, name_source?, name_at?, photo?, photo_source?, photo_at?, notes?, created_at, updated_at)   -- photo: BLOB, the accepted card's encoded photo as received (at most 24,576 bytes, HUMAN-CHAT.md's bound), decoded at display with the memory-safe decoder at the fixed size, never a reference; it replaces avatar_ref, which v9 drops, since ADR-0059 decision 5 makes a photo bytes and never a reference (A 2026-10-22, ADR-0059 (iv)); local_endpoint_id: the identity that accepted the contact and alone sees it (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0061 decisions 4 and 9) — the column is schema v14's (below; `crates/human/store` `schema.rs`), not schema v9's (A 2026-10-25); name_source/photo_source: card | person, with when (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0059 decision 3): a local edit is never overwritten by a card, and the contact page says where a value came from; content-free, in this review
contact_routes(contact_id, local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id, identity_key?, device_label?, verification_note?, last_seen?)   -- a route is one remote user identity held by one local identity (A 2026-10-21 (ii), ADR-0061 decision 4, ADR-0059 decisions 3-4); identity_key: absent until a card is accepted on the route (typed contacts and every route from before ADR-0063 have none), set by the first accepted card, replaced only by the person's accepted key change until the release that builds Stage 22, from which ADR-0067 decision 5 makes a differing key another route and no replacement happens; the local identities' own private keys live outside this store, one sealed file per identity under the state root; local_endpoint_id is its contact's, carried here so the rule is one index; one contact per (local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id) and at most one route per peer_id under a contact — two endpoints of one PeerId are two contacts of that identity, shown as sharing a node, and another identity's contacts are never consulted; enforced by the store's writers (plan §29 step 2), the two indexes it implies — UNIQUE(local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id) and UNIQUE(contact_id, peer_id) — and FOREIGN KEY(contact_id, local_endpoint_id) REFERENCES contacts(contact_id, local_endpoint_id) ON DELETE CASCADE, backed by UNIQUE(contact_id, local_endpoint_id) on contacts (the parent key SQLite requires), so a route cannot sit under another identity's contact, added in the §Logical tables review, where existing contacts, contact_routes and conversation_index rows are backfilled with the first identity's derived EndpointId as their local identity — the column, the composite foreign key and that backfill are schema v14's (below): from schema v9 until then the table carried `UNIQUE(contact_id, peer_id, endpoint_id)`, `UNIQUE(contact_id, peer_id)` and the single-column foreign key, so a contact held at most one route per PeerId and one row per route by the schema, while one contact per route was the writers' rule alone (A 2026-10-25)
conversation_index(conversation_id, local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id?, channel_id?, title?, last_activity?, card_shared_at?)   -- local_endpoint_id: the identity the conversation speaks as, for life (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0061 decision 3; the column schema v14's, as above), and with conversation_id the row's key, PRIMARY KEY(local_endpoint_id, conversation_id), so two identities each hold their own row for one remote route or one channel; card_shared_at: when that identity's card was last shared (ADR-0059 decision 2, A (ii); it replaces last_shared_card, since the card is the identity's); both content-free, in the §Logical tables review
own_cards(endpoint_id, name, surname?, photo?, created_at, updated_at)   -- A 2026-10-21 (ii), ADR-0059 decision 2, ADR-0061 decision 1: one card per user identity, keyed by its EndpointId, which since ADR-0067 (A 2026-10-28) is derived from the identity's own key — the identity's display name and photo, written by the person, outside ADR-0044's three states; the row is the identity's presentation, not its standing (ADR-0061 decision 1: the first identity is the authored human endpoint, every other one an endpoint in the overlay's `added` member); a discard of the store (ADR-0060 decision 6) loses the rows and the next open recreates one per identity with the route label as its name
pending_outbound(local_row_id, local_endpoint_id, app_message_id, transport_message_id,
                 destination_peer?, destination_endpoint?, channel_id?, media_type?, payload,
                 created_at, last_attempt_at?, retry_state)
unread_inbound(local_row_id, local_endpoint_id, app_message_id, source_peer, source_endpoint?,
               channel_id?, media_type?, payload, received_at, kind)
kept_inbound(local_row_id, local_endpoint_id, app_message_id, source_peer, source_endpoint?,
             channel_id?, media_type?, payload, received_at, read_at, kept_at, kind)
settings(key, value)
read_pairs(local_endpoint_id, origin, app_message_id, at)   -- local_endpoint_id on these four tables is schema v14's (A 2026-11-19, below); until that date this block showed a conversation_id on the three message tables, which no schema carried
```

Schema v10 (A 2026-10-27, the start window of `design/2026-10-27-start-window.md`, rust-ui-dev-02's j68; architect-cto's rulings of the same day to the four questions of its first batch) adds two derived, content-free columns and the indexes the start reads need, so that opening the app reads counts and summaries, never every row. `contacts.name_key` is the comparison key of the contact's display name as the store computes it (`store::name_key`: NFKC, case fold, white space, the confusables skeleton — the case key of contact-cards design §5.4.1, the one the store holds today; the design's other two keys are schema v12's columns beside it, under the same version), so that a card's standing against the contacts is a handful of indexed queries rather than every contact decoded per card; it reconstructs nothing the row's `display_name` does not already hold. `kind` on `unread_inbound` and `kept_inbound` is the row's envelope kind as the client's own classification reads it at commit — `text`, `card`, or `other` for a row this build cannot show — so that the unread badge is one indexed count of the decodable kinds and the hidden count the rest, with no row decoded at start; it is RETENTION.md §5's content-free metadata (an index, not content), and `other` names a row, never what it holds. A third rule of the same batch's second half (A 2026-10-27 (ii), rust-ui-dev-02's A1): `conversation_index` holds a row for every direct conversation the store holds rows of — written in the same transaction as the first pending, unread or kept row of that conversation (`INSERT OR IGNORE`), where until then only a shared card wrote one — and an open-time backfill under a `settings` row `conversation_index_version` indexes the direct conversations of every stored pending, unread and kept row once; a peer already past 64 from before keeps what it holds, and only a new endpoint of it is refused. A channel conversation is keyed by its channel and is outside the per-peer bound, which is about a peer's choice of labels. All three are versioned by a `settings` row each (`name_key_version`, `inbound_kind_version`, `conversation_index_version`): a build whose normalisation or classification differs recomputes every key, or reclassifies every row of an older version, at open before any count is read, once per upgrade, bounded by the store's own size. The indexes: `contacts (name_key)` — the same-name standing is asked by the key, and without it every card scans every contact, the cost v10 removes; the contacts table had no `local_endpoint_id` column before schema v14 (above), which adds the column, scopes contacts by identity (ADR-0061 decision 4) and widens this index to `(local_endpoint_id, name_key)` in the same change — `contact_routes (peer_id, endpoint_id)`, `contact_routes (identity_key)`, the two inbound tables' paging by direct conversation, `(source_peer, source_endpoint_key, received_at, row_id)`, and by channel, `(channel_key, received_at, row_id)` — the two `_key` columns being the tables' generated non-null forms of `source_endpoint` and `channel_id`, which key a conversation; the inbound tables carry no conversation column of their own — and two COVERING indexes for the counts read at start, so that the badge's count per conversation and both hidden counts are answered from an index and read no row (a count that touches a row reads its payload's overflow chain under the cipher, measured 10 to 17 times slower by #489's review): `unread_inbound (kind, source_peer, source_endpoint, channel_id, app_message_id)` — over the table's own columns, not the generated `_key` forms, since SQLite recomputes a virtual column from its row rather than reading the index's copy, which makes the index a search and not a cover (measured by #489's plan test: `USING COVERING INDEX` over the own columns, `USING INDEX` over the `_key` forms); the counts group by `source_peer, source_endpoint` where `channel_id` is null and by `channel_id` otherwise, which is the `_key` grouping for every row the client writes — and `kept_inbound (kind)`, which holds only its hidden count. The unread badge counts the unread rows of a decodable kind in the conversation, one per row (A 2026-10-27 (ii), the owner's second review of the client, judged by the review class at 1b37e0ef): the table's key already scopes a row by its publisher and, for a direct message, its source endpoint, so no two unread rows of one conversation are one message, and two publishers reusing one id on a channel are two rows, two items in the conversation (the model keys a channel item by its publisher, as `read_pairs` below keys a pair) and two in the badge. The sentence first read "distinct `(conversation_id, app_message_id)`", with a kept-item case as its reason; that case the count, read over `unread_inbound` alone, never meets, and the distinct count merged two publishers' rows on a channel while the page showed both, so the store's query and its test changed with this sentence in the start window's second batch (#492, 62d11cb5: both counts `COUNT(*)` over the same covering index, whose trailing `app_message_id` the count no longer needs and v10 keeps), before any screen read the count. Reading an item deletes its unread row as RETENTION.md says. Pending rows keep no count ceiling: a ceiling is a product rule of a later note (the design's §13 Q1), not this fix's. The distinct remote endpoints the store holds per remote PeerId are at most 64 (HUMAN-CHAT.md's malformed rule, A 2026-10-27): the count is the union of `conversation_index`'s rows and `contact_routes`' rows for that peer — from schema v14 for that peer under one local identity, each identity counted alone (A 2026-11-19, below) — so it is durable, bounded by the store, and an endpoint, once indexed, is forgotten by a discard alone (A 2026-10-27 (ii): no operation removes a `conversation_index` row — a conversation is its rows and the index outlives them, and a route's removal forgets nothing — so neither emptying a conversation nor deleting a route resets the bound, and a discard clears all; the one case the build did not hold at #492, its round 3 F6 — an endpoint known through a route alone, which a store upgraded from a build that did not index on commit could hold, forgotten with its route — is closed by 7deee00d: accepting a card indexes the route's pair in its own transaction, and the index's version 2 adds every route's pair at open, tested by deleting a contact accepted this build and one backfilled from version 1 and finding the endpoint still counted); the transport client checks it before it commits a direct inbound and before a direct send writes its pending row (`crates/human/transport-client`, `admits_endpoint`). The person's own send to a direct endpoint indexes its conversation and counts toward that peer's 64 like an inbound; a send that would open a 65th is refused by the same check before the pending row is written, shown as any refused send is (Not sent, with the reason), its person-facing words ux-designer's if the state needs more than that.

Schema v11 (A 2026-10-27, #492's review F2; built in the start window's second batch with the Chats list's read, 8de17d31, rust-ui-dev-02's) widens `kept_inbound_by_kind` from `(kind)` to `(kind, source_peer, source_endpoint, channel_id)` — the shape `unread_inbound_by_kind` has without its trailing `app_message_id`, over the table's own columns for the reason v10 gives — so that the set of conversations the Chats list shows, kept-only ones included (`design/2026-10-27-start-window.md` §3), is read from the two `_by_kind` indexes alone with no kept row read; a conversation's newest row and its time are the first row of the per-conversation paging indexes, not this one's. An index rebuild only: no row is written and no version row is needed, and the plan test that refuses any non-covering step on either inbound table (#492) is what holds it.

Schema v12 (A 2026-10-31, rust-ui-dev-02's j60, QUESTION 01a19122 ruled by DECISION 01a19125-a00a; contact-cards design §5.4.1 as #438 rewrote it, `human-client-ui.md` §13 bullets 4 and 9) adds the design's other two comparison keys beside `name_key`, each derived from `display_name` as `name_key` is and content-free for the same reason: `shape_key` (white space, the confusables skeleton, the case fold, the skeleton again) and `compat_shape_key` (NFKC, then the shape key's steps); `name_key` stays the case key. Two names are the same when any one of the three keys is equal, each key compared only with the same key of the other name, so the same-name standing is the union of three indexed equalities — `contacts_by_shape_key` and `contacts_by_compat_shape_key` beside `contacts_by_name_key` — and never a scan; the rule is not transitive and nothing reads it as if it were, since the warning and §6.1's titles compare one pair at a time. The two columns start at their empty default and carry no version row of their own: `name_key_version` covers all three (v10's "under the same version"), bumped to 2 by this step so that the first open derives every contact's three keys before any standing reads one, and a later change to any one key's output bumps it again and re-derives all three. The ui-model's twin titles (§13 bullet 9) ask the store's same-name predicate and compare nothing by a weaker rule themselves. Schema v14 widens all three indexes to `(local_endpoint_id, <key>)`, as v10 says of `contacts_by_name_key`. The design's item 7 pairs are the store's test vectors, one per key that joins them and one pair no key joins.

Schema v13 (A 2026-11-03, rust-ui-dev-01's j196, the reply-target lookup of `design/2026-10-27-start-window.md` §5.2 accepted as an interim for B4c; architect-cto's DECISION of the same day to rust-ui-dev-01's QUESTION 01a1a080, answering (a) of its three) adds one covering index per inbound table, `unread_inbound_by_message` and `kept_inbound_by_message`, over `(app_message_id, channel_id, source_peer, source_endpoint, kind)` — the tables' own columns, for v10's reason — so that a reply whose target the app does not hold asks the store whether the target is stored in this conversation as a row the app can show (§5.2, §6.1: `text` or `card`, never `other`) by one probe per table that reads the index alone, never a row and never the payload. The two shapes a conversation takes decide the column order: a direct conversation is `(source_peer, source_endpoint)` with `channel_id` null (the index holds the table's own nullable column, not the generated `channel_key`) and probes all five columns; a channel conversation is `channel_id` alone, whoever published, and probes `app_message_id` and `channel_id`, taking `kind` from the index entry. The tables' UNIQUE key `(source_peer, source_endpoint_key, channel_key, app_message_id)` already answers the direct probe but reads the row for `kind` (v10 appended it after `payload`), and cannot be probed for a channel's target without its publisher: the planner then ranges the channel's rows through `*_by_channel`, the payload-reading shape v10 refused, growing with the channel (measured 2026-11-03 at 500 rows of 48 KiB, warm: 0.168 ms by the range against 0.022 ms by the covering index; the direct probe 0.056 ms against 0.017 ms). A row of the same id under another conversation is not reached by either probe, which is §5.2's "counts as not stored". The migration creates the two indexes and writes no row; no column changes, and `name_key_version` is untouched. This paragraph first said that Stage 22 would widen neither index, a conversation's local identity being read from `conversation_index` before the probe; that was wrong, since two identities' conversations with one remote route are told apart by nothing on the inbound row, and schema v14 leads both indexes with `local_endpoint_id` (A 2026-11-19, below).

Schema v14 (A 2026-11-19, Stage 22's migration and plan §30's §Logical tables review; rust-ui-dev-02's review of `schema.rs` as it stood at v13, their j125, ruled by architect-cto the same day; built by the store's Stage 22 change and by nothing before it) is the step that gives every row its local identity. `contacts`, `contact_routes` and `conversation_index` gain `local_endpoint_id` with the keys their rows above state — `conversation_index` keyed by `(local_endpoint_id, conversation_id)` — and so do the four tables no earlier text named: `pending_outbound`, `unread_inbound`, `kept_inbound` and `read_pairs`. A conversation is told apart from another identity's with the same remote route, or the same channel, by that column and by nothing else a row holds, so the column is on the row — and not a `conversation_id`, which the inbound tables do not carry (v10) and which would repeat three columns in every row and index. It leads the UNIQUE key of the two inbound tables, `(local_endpoint_id, source_peer, source_endpoint_key, channel_key, app_message_id)`, and of `read_pairs`, so that one message that reached two identities is two rows, and reading it under one suppresses no copy under the other; it leads the inbound tables' `_by_conversation`, `_by_channel`, `_by_kind` and `_by_message` indexes, each otherwise as its own version left it, so that every count and probe of v10, v11 and v13 is asked for one identity and stays covered; and the three name indexes of `contacts` widen as v10 and v12 say. `pending_outbound` takes the column and the index `pending_outbound_by_identity (local_endpoint_id, created_at, row_id)`, an identity's pending rows in the order they were written, and keeps its two UNIQUE keys, `app_message_id` and `transport_message_id`, unscoped: both are this node's own mint, and a transport id scoped by identity could be written twice. `read_pairs` is bounded per identity from this version, at most 4096 rows under each `local_endpoint_id`, the oldest of that identity evicted by its newest: one bound for the store would let one identity's reads evict another's pairs, and a copy re-sent to the other would return as unread for something done in an identity not in front (ADR-0061 decision 9). Remove of an identity (ADR-0061 decision 7) is then one indexed delete per table. Existing rows take the first identity's EndpointId, the one derived from its key, which the client hands to the open since the store reads no configuration; what becomes of a stored remote label is §Migrations'. A channel conversation is `{local_endpoint_id, channel_id}` (`human-client-ui.md` §4), and two identities may each hold one of the same channel: a join reference is the connection's (LOCAL-IPC.md §Push events and overload), so each identity's session joins its own channels, takes its own copy of a post and commits its own row, with its own read and Keep state; an identity that has not joined holds nothing of the channel, nothing is written for it from another identity's rows (ADR-0061 decision 9), and a post is stored at most once per identity that joined, which is the person's own choice of joins and no publisher's. The per-peer bound of v10 is counted per identity from this version: at most 64 distinct remote endpoints per remote PeerId under one local identity, over that identity's `conversation_index` and `contact_routes` rows alone, answered for the index rows by `conversation_index_by_peer (local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id)`, which covers the count, and not from the text of a `conversation_id`, whose form is the store's own; Remove of an identity deletes its index rows, the second operation, beside a discard, that forgets an endpoint.

A replacement (A 2026-11-22; ADR-0067 decision 5, ADR-0061 decision 7; ruled for ux-designer's design of it, built by the store's change for that design and by nothing before it) adds two records, both content-free, whose table and column names are the build's. **The replaces record**: one row saying that the identity with one local EndpointId replaces the identity with another. It is written after the client generates the new identity's key pair, from which the id is known, and before the key file is sealed, the platform key made or the administrative add of the new endpoint sent, and when it cannot be written none of the three is done (A 2026-11-26 (ii)): a process that ends between the add and a later write would otherwise leave an added identity nothing ties to the old one, and one that ends between the seal and a later write a key file no row names, and in both a second replacement would mint another key and make a third identity. A row whose key was never sealed is settled as any row whose new identity the list lacks is (ADR-0061 decision 7): the same add is sent, is refused for the missing key, and the row is discarded. The row holds no alias and no client kinds: the add sent again names the row's key, takes the alias the client would choose for a new identity then and an identity's client kinds, and where an entry left ahead holds another alias the add is refused and the row discarded, that discard's remove taking the entry out. At most one row names an old identity, a replacement minting no key while one does, so the rows are bounded by the profile's 64 endpoints. It is deleted in the transaction that removes either identity, so a removed identity is named by no row; for the first identity, which is turned off and never removed, it stays while the new one exists. **The share-again list**: the remote routes, `{peer_id, endpoint_id}` with their order, of the old identity's conversations in which its card was or may have been shared, stored under the new identity's `local_endpoint_id` before the old identity's rows are deleted. It holds no title and no message: a row's title is read from the new identity's contacts, which the replacement copied from the old one's, else it is the route. A row is deleted when the new card is shared on it, all of them when the person closes the list, and all of them with the new identity's Remove; the list is bounded, the bound stated with the build. The copy of contacts is ordinary `contacts` and `contact_routes` rows under the new identity; the trusted set is the overlay's, not the store's (ADR-0067 decision 6). Neither record was in the store as Stage 22's batch stood on 2026-11-23 (A 2026-11-23; rust-ui-dev-02's INFO 01a20a42, the store supply at cf88710ed): schema v14 as built created no table for either, and no act of the client minted a replacement's key, so this paragraph was the target and described no build. The two records arrive with the store change that builds the client's replacement act; plan §30 step 2 is where its not being built is said.

The pending-add record (A 2026-11-26; ADR-0061 decision 7, ruled for ux-designer's design of the add form, `design/2026-11-04-identities.md` §6.2) is a third record of the same kind, content-free, its table and column names the build's: one row naming the local EndpointId of an identity the add form has asked for and the client does not yet know to exist. It is written after the form generates the key pair, from which the id is known, and before the key file is sealed, the platform key made or the administrative add sent, and when it cannot be written none of the three is done; at most 64 rows, the profile's bound on endpoints, a write past it being a row that cannot be written and no row evicted to make room, a row being the one thing that names its key (A 2026-11-26 (ii)): a key sealed first, by a process that then ended, would be a key file no row names, which nothing deletes; a key kept after an add whose outcome is unknown is recognised by this row and by nothing else, since `endpoint-keys/` also holds keys a deployment minted (ADR-0067 decision 4) and a key file whose id no list holds is not thereby the form's. It holds no alias, no client kinds, no name and no photo; the form's values are the session's. It is deleted when the id is an identity (the add answered `added` or `unchanged`, or an endpoint list that answered holds the id while its key file is there), after the key file and the platform key of a form left on a known refusal are deleted, and at the end of a discard, once the administrative remove answered `removed` or `EndpointUnknown`; on any other answer, and on a list that failed, it stays and is settled again at the client's start and whenever the list of identities opens. The clean-up of an identity that cannot come back (ADR-0061 decision 7) never deletes it, and it is no identity's row: an id it names has no card, no conversation and no contact until the id is an identity, which it is to the client from the row's deletion and not before (A 2026-11-26 (ii)): while a row names an id the client opens no data session and holds no lease for it, so nothing is admitted under it, also where the add answered `added` and the row's delete then failed; that delete is tried again at every settlement, whose first case (the id listed, its key file there) ends the state. So a settlement that discards a listed id whose key path is empty deletes no conversation and no contact, none having been written. A replacement's new key is named by the replaces record and by no row here. A discard of the store (A 2026-11-26 (ii)) takes these rows and the replaces records with every other row, settles none of them first and deletes no file in `endpoint-keys/`: the discard that ends a record is the record's own, of its key path, platform key and endpoint. After it an id the endpoint list holds, then or after the daemon's next start, is an identity with its card recreated from its alias, an old identity and its replacement are two identities nothing ties, and a key file whose add never wrote stays in `endpoint-keys/` unused; no mark of these rows is kept outside the store, which would be a second record of what the store held, and the directory is not scanned. The record is not in the store (schema v14 creates no table for it, and no add form is built in the client as of this note); it arrives with the store change that builds the form, and its migration is that change's.

`read_pairs` (A 2026-10-03, Stage 15 Q5) is the bounded duplicate-suppression record RETENTION.md §5 allows: a content-free pair naming where a message came from (`origin`: its source peer and endpoint for a direct message, its source peer and channel for a broadcast — the store's own inbound identity, so two peers reusing one id on a channel are two pairs, never one) and the application id the sender gave it, with the time the pair was written — nothing else, pinned by the store's shape guard. It is written when a row is marked read and when Keep is removed, so a copy of a message that was read and not kept, re-sent after a restart, does not reappear as unread; a copy carrying the same id with different text is suppressed too, because the id is the sender's claim that it is the same message and a content-free table must take it at its word. At most 4096 rows under each local identity (schema v14, above; one bound for the whole table until then); an identity's oldest is evicted inside the transaction that inserts its newest. It joins the owner's privacy review beside `conversation_index` and `contact_routes.last_seen`.

One client process, several identities (A 2026-11-25; ruled for rust-ui-dev-02's step 1 wiring, plan §30's note of that day). The process opens the store once: every identity's handle is that one connection together with its `local_endpoint_id`, and no identity opens a second. A release must take the released pages out of the write-ahead log (RETENTION.md), and a second connection of the same process, holding a statement open for another identity, can make that checkpoint answer busy, which would turn one identity's read into another's failed release; it is also one store, one custody and one key in memory (ADR-0061 decision 1). Store health is therefore the store's and one for every identity: when it cannot commit unread content, every identity releases its endpoint (§Store health). The identity last in front is one content-free row of `settings`, the EndpointId and nothing else, written at the switch and lost with a discard; with no row, or a row naming an id the client no longer holds, the profile's default is in front when it is one of the client's identities, else the first identity. Until a join per identity exists, the first identity's session alone joins the profile's `channels.desired`: v14 made every existing channel row the first identity's, and an identity that has joined nothing holds nothing of a channel, as v14 says; the two-identity channel case of v14 is reached by that join and by nothing before it.

A current-session RAM model may also hold transport-terminal outbound messages and read-but-unkept inbound messages for display. Those rows are not serialized as general message history.

No transport private key, trust allowlist, endpoint lease, Kademlia bucket, relay reservation, AutoNAT evidence, transport direct-message dedup record (ADR-0019 — `read_pairs` above is the application's, keyed on the application id), or endpoint-directory cache is stored here.

## Retention transitions

- user sends -> durable `pending_outbound` is committed **before** the first transport call;
- direct `AcceptedV2` -> delete durable pending content;
- successful broadcast publication -> delete durable pending content, without claiming recipient delivery;
- inbound event consumed -> durable `unread_inbound` is committed before normal notification/UI presentation;
- inbound becomes locally read -> delete durable unread content unless the receiver explicitly chooses `Keep`;
- receiver chooses `Keep` after read -> durable `kept_inbound`;
- receiver removes `Keep` -> delete durable kept content.

Remote data can never set the Keep state.

## Message-status honesty

Application state may record:

- local send requested / pending;
- transport accepted remotely for direct;
- broadcast locally published;
- transport rejected/timeout;
- local inbound received;
- local UI read state;
- local receiver Keep state.

It must not label transport acceptance as “read by human” or “processed by app” without a future application-level receipt protocol. Local `read`/`Keep` are not sent remotely in v1.

## Where the store lives

The database and the single-instance lock beside it sit in the human client's own directory under the profile's state root — `ProfilePaths::human_dir()`, `<state_dir>/human` (A 2026-10-03, Stage 15 Q3) — never among the daemon's files (its sockets, profile lock and identity key), and the profile's `identity.key_file` may not point inside it — judged on disk, links followed (`configuration.md`; the lexical check landed with Stage 15's batch 2, the on-disk one A 2026-10-05). Deleting the directory loses only what RETENTION.md allows to be lost. One client file outside it is the sealed store key, `<state_dir>/human-store.key` (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0060 decision 3): kept out of `human/` so that an export or a copy of the store's directory leaves the key behind, and kept out of the daemon's identity directory because the daemon's secrets are not the client's; deleting `human/` leaves it, harmless and useless alone, and recreating the store replaces it. The others are the endpoints' sealed key files under `<state_dir>/endpoint-keys/` (ADR-0063 decision 2's `identity-keys/` until the Stage 22 release; ADR-0067 names the directory for every endpoint, and a file is adopted into it, not renamed — its header names the endpoint's id, so `transportctl endpoint adopt` on the desktop and the embedded host's re-seal on Android write the new file and remove the old, ADR-0067 decision 4, A 2026-11-19): outside `human/` for the same reasons, and unlike the store key they survive a discard and recreate of the store — every identity keeps its key and fingerprint; Remove of the identity deletes its file, and a Keystore invalidation leaves it unusable until the person creates a new identity (ADR-0063 decision 2 as ADR-0067 decision 5 reads it: a new key is a new EndpointId).

## Store health

Unread durability is a human-client property, not a transport guarantee. If SQLite is unavailable/full/corrupt such that new unread content cannot be committed, the human client exposes degraded storage health, releases/disables its direct EndpointId, and suspends its local human broadcast joins/delivery until storage is healthy rather than present itself as a normally durable receiver. Profile `channels.desired` may remain network-prewarmed without local delivery. The quota a profile may set on the store's pages (the desktop's `--store-max-pages`, SQLite's `max_page_count`) bounds what the network writes, never what the build writes to its own store (A 2026-10-27 (ii), #492's review F3, carried as P3): the writes an open performs — a migration, a key or kind refresh, an index backfill — run above the quota, which is re-applied once they are done, since each is bounded by the store's own size and none is a peer's doing; a store over the quota after them opens degraded as this paragraph says, its content readable and releasable and no new row committed; one exactly at it opens healthy, since SQLite takes a ceiling the file already fills, and only a write past it cannot be committed; and a migration that fails for any other reason is recovery mode (Migrations, below), never degraded. Built as one fix covering all three writes (fd51dedf: the migration, the shape check, the key refresh and the index backfill run before the quota is applied, a store over it afterwards opens degraded, and a zero quota is refused before anything is written; tested on a store vacuumed to its quota that needs the backfill, which opens degraded with every row readable and indexed, and fails as disk-full under the order before it), closing the gap in which such an open refused.

There remains a bounded handoff window after transport endpoint-queue acceptance and before human-store commit. `AcceptedV2` is not redefined.

## Migrations

Every schema migration is transactional and versioned. Migration failure puts the human app database into recovery/read-only/export mode; it never triggers transport identity regeneration. Stage 15's desktop client ships the recovery half (A 2026-10-03, Q4): a blocking screen with no session and no lease, showing the database path, offering Quit and Retry, and never renaming, moving or deleting the file; the read-only open and the export are carried by name (plan §18). One step is file-level, by exception (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0060 decision 6): the rewrite of a plaintext `human.sqlite` into an encrypted one — seal and sync the store key, export to `human.sqlite.encrypting`, rename it to `human.sqlite.encrypted` as the durable mark of a complete copy, scrub the plaintext in place while it holds its name, unlink its `-wal`, `-shm` and then the file, rename the encrypted file into place, each with a directory sync — with its crash states told apart by name on the next open: plaintext beside `.encrypting` restarts the rewrite from the intact plaintext, plaintext beside `.encrypted` keeps the copy and resumes the scrub, `.encrypted` alone finishes the rename, a `human.sqlite` that opens under the key is the store; the platform reporting the key invalidated offers discard-and-recreate directly; anything else — a present key that does not open the file, no key file, an unlisted combination of names — goes to recovery mode, in that order. From this date the recovery screen offers a third, confirmed action, **Discard and recreate**, beside Quit and Retry — the one relaxation of its never-delete rule, on the person's word only — because on the desktop a lost key file and a damaged never-migrated store leave the same files and the screen must not trap the person. The recovery screen's never-rename rule is about a store that failed to migrate, and does not apply to this rewrite.

Schema v14 (A 2026-11-19; ADR-0067 decision 7, amended that day) is where ADR-0067's clean break reaches the store: after it no table holds a label, and no column marks a row as from before. In its one transaction: (a) every row takes the first identity's derived EndpointId as its local identity, and the `own_cards` row is rekeyed from the old label to that id — where a row keyed by that id already exists it stays and every label row is deleted, none rekeyed; of several label rows, which no build wrote, the most recently updated is the one rekeyed and the others are deleted; a row keyed by another derived id is another identity's and stays; and none of this is counted, a card being the identity's presentation and outside RETENTION.md §1 (A 2026-11-22); (b) a `contact_routes` row with an `identity_key` is rebound — its `endpoint_id` becomes the id that key derives — and every row keyed by that `{peer, label}` pair follows it: the `conversation_index` row with its `card_shared_at`, the unread and kept rows' `source_endpoint` and the pair's `read_pairs`, a channel's rows among them, whose `source_endpoint` is the endpoint their publisher asserted (A 2026-11-23); (c) a `{peer, label}` pair that no keyed route names — a conversation with no accepted card, an automated client's endpoint, a route from before ADR-0063, and a pair whose endpoint already has a derived id's form while no keyed route names it, which no released build writes: the test is the route's key and never what the endpoint's text parses as (A 2026-11-22) — is discarded: its `conversation_index` row, its unread, kept and pending rows, its `read_pairs` and any route without a key, and a contact left with no route is deleted, as deleting a contact's last route is the contact's deletion (ADR-0059 decision 9), while a contact that held no route before the migration lost none in it and stays (A 2026-11-22); a channel's row is no part of (c) — where no keyed route names its publisher's `{peer, label}` the row stays, and its `source_endpoint` is set to NULL in `unread_inbound`, `kept_inbound` and `read_pairs`: the label was the publisher's assertion and a display hint, the PeerId is who published, and the row is content RETENTION.md holds, so it is discarded for nothing and counted nowhere, while a reader that took a stored label as absent would leave the label in the row's UNIQUE key and miss the row by the origin it handed out (A 2026-11-23; rust-ui-dev-02's QUESTION 01a20a60 on p2p-network-dev-01's finding, ruled that day: as the gap was found, the build rebound and discarded direct rows alone and a channel's row kept its label); a pair set to NULL this way is not met by a copy its publisher sends again under a derived id, which returns as unread once, a limit named and accepted; (d) two routes of one PeerId whose keys derive one id are one identity: the route stays under the contact created first (`created_at`, then `contact_id`) and the other route is deleted, its contact with it when it holds no other; the two conversations become one, with one `conversation_index` row carrying the later `card_shared_at`; their unread and kept rows all follow to the one id, and where two of them then meet on the key the one received earlier stays and the other is discarded, an unread row that meets a kept row of the same message going in the kept row's favour, since the person read and kept it, and an unread row that meets a `read_pairs` row of the same message discarded as an arriving copy of it would be (RETENTION.md §5: read and not kept stays gone); where two `read_pairs` meet, the earlier stays; a meeting is between the rows of two routes, or between a channel's rows that (b)'s rebind or (c)'s NULL brought to one key — one publisher's one `app_message_id` on one channel under two labels, or under a label and none, which no build of ours writes and a peer could have sent — and nothing else, the channel's meetings settled by these same rules within a table and across them, so an unread copy beside its own route's kept row, a state v13 allows, stays as it was — the migration settles what its rebinding and its NULL make meet and no other state it finds (A 2026-11-22; the channel's rows A 2026-11-23); and one `{peer, label}` pair held under two contacts with two different keys, which no writer of the store makes, fails the migration rather than being settled either way, since either choice would show one key's texts under the other key's contact: the transaction is rolled back, the store stays at v13 and the open is refused as any failed migration is, above (A 2026-11-22); (e) every `pending_outbound` row is discarded, direct and channel alike, a rebound conversation's included: a text the previous release encoded carries its sender's label as `from_endpoint` and a card is signed over that label, so the stored bytes, resent, would be accepted by the transport and dropped as malformed by a receiver on this release, a loss shown to the sender as a delivery, and the store holds no key to encode them again — counted in two parts, as Remove of an identity names them (ADR-0061 decision 7): the rows never attempted, which were not sent, and the rows with an attempt behind them, which may have reached their counterparty under the previous release (RETENTION.md §2's `attempts > 0`) and are said as that, never as not sent; and every unread row of kind `card` is discarded, since a proposal signed over a label cannot verify against the id its row now names, and the counterparty's card, shared again, is a new proposal (the accepted contact is untouched: it is the `contacts` row, not the card). Stored payloads are not rewritten, and the unread and kept texts that stay are read as they were: the release's read of a STORED row takes a `from_endpoint` that does not parse as absent — on a direct row it never had authority (HUMAN-CHAT.md), on a channel row it was a display hint, now not shown — while an envelope off the wire with such a `from_endpoint` stays malformed, so only a row committed before v14 can meet the rule; `inbound_kind_version` is bumped, and the reclassification runs under that stored-row read, so a text of the previous release stays `text`. What (c), (d) and (e) discarded is counted into one content-free `settings` row, each count holding one thing a person would call gone or changed (A 2026-11-19 (ii), on ux-designer's design of the notice): conversations, the distinct `{peer, endpoint}` pairs (c) discarded, a merge under (d) losing none; unread messages and kept messages, the rows (c) discarded, an unread row of kind `card` not among them; pending never attempted and pending that may have reached, every `pending_outbound` row the migration discarded in those two parts, whichever of (c) and (e) took it (A 2026-11-22 (ii): the count is by the row, since (c) discards the pending rows of each conversation it removes and no order of the steps is fixed); contacts, those (c) deleted with their last route; contacts combined, those (d) deleted because their route went to another contact of the same identity, whose own name and notes went with them while the person's contact stays; and card proposals, every unread row of kind `card` the migration discarded, whichever of (c) and (e) took it (A 2026-11-22 (ii)), which are no messages and which the counterparty's card, shared again, replaces. The second copy of a message under (d), an unread row that met a kept row or a `read_pairs` row there, and the later of two `read_pairs` are counted nowhere: the message is still held, or was read and not kept, so nothing the person had went. The row is written only when a count is not zero (A 2026-11-22): it exists to be acknowledged, and a migration that discarded nothing asks nothing of the person. The client shows the row's non-zero counts at each open until the person acknowledges them, and deletes the row on that acknowledgement and on nothing else, so a process that ends at the first open loses no notice; the words are ux-designer's. The showing is not built with the migration (A 2026-11-24): the Stage 22 batch writes the row and gives the store its read and its delete, and no client calls either, so until rust-ui-dev's notice lands (plan §30, the note of 2026-11-24; `human-client-ui.md` §13) the row stays as written and the person is told nothing; since the acknowledgement alone deletes it, the build that brings the notice still finds it and shows it then. These are the only places a migration deletes content RETENTION.md §1 holds — (c)'s conversations, the second copy of a message under (d), and (e)'s pending rows and card proposals; they are ADR-0067's break and no precedent, and no later migration may discard a row RETENTION.md holds. The first identity's id is handed to the open by the client, so a profile the release refuses at load leaves the store at v13 and unopened; the rebuild of `contacts` beneath its routes runs with foreign keys off for the transaction and checked before its commit, still one transaction; and the key's adoption into `endpoint-keys/`, the seed of the first identity's trusted set (ADR-0067 decision 6) and the `advertise` delta are writes outside SQLite, recorded nowhere in the store and waited on by nothing in it.

A migration from any prototype/general-history schema must not preserve full history by default. It must explicitly classify rows into pending-outbound, unread-inbound, receiver-kept-inbound, or discard message content according to ADR-0044.

## Backup policy

Android system backup/device transfer excludes the entire human-store database, its sealed store key and the rest of the application's data, permanently (ADR-0060 decision 8, the owner's word of 2026-10-21): InterWeave data never leaves the device through a platform backup, a platform transfer or any external provider; the backup that will exist travels over InterWeave itself between the person's own devices, by a later record; until then a new device starts from the recovery phrase and an empty store.

A future explicit encrypted application backup may include **message content only from `unread_inbound` and `kept_inbound`**. `pending_outbound` is local crash/restart state but is excluded from portable backup to prevent restored/second devices from becoming implicit replay/delayed-send sources.

Contacts/configuration backup remains a separate policy and does not change message-retention eligibility.
