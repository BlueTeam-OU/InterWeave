# Human-client application state and persistence

Status: design only. Message-content retention is normative through [`RETENTION.md`](./RETENTION.md) and ADR-0044.

## Selected store

Use a versioned SQLite database behind a Rust `human-store` crate on desktop and Android. The application storage schema is independent of transport configuration/state and may be deleted/rebuilt without changing PeerId or trust policy.

The store is **not** a conventional permanent conversation-history database. Message content is durable only in the retention states defined by `RETENTION.md`.

The store is encrypted at rest as a whole — pages, write-ahead log and journal — under a per-profile store key the platform's custody seals (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0060): on Android an `AndroidKeyStore`-wrapped key under the `background-compatible` policy, on the v1 desktop one owner-only sealed key file `<state_dir>/human-store.key`, under the state root and outside this directory so a copy of `<state_dir>/human` leaves it behind, at ADR-0038's `filesystem-only` level, moving into ADR-0038's envelope when it lands. The mechanism is SPIKE-011's to select within ADR-0060 decision 4; an invalidated key is a store-health condition with discard-and-recreate offered directly; a missing key or a damaged file goes to recovery mode (§Migrations), never an identity condition.

## Logical tables

Conceptually:

```text
contacts(contact_id, local_endpoint_id, display_name, name_key, name_source?, name_at?, photo?, photo_source?, photo_at?, notes?, created_at, updated_at)   -- photo: BLOB, the accepted card's encoded photo as received (at most 24,576 bytes, HUMAN-CHAT.md's bound), decoded at display with the memory-safe decoder at the fixed size, never a reference; it replaces avatar_ref, which v9 drops, since ADR-0059 decision 5 makes a photo bytes and never a reference (A 2026-10-22, ADR-0059 (iv)); local_endpoint_id: the identity that accepted the contact and alone sees it (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0061 decisions 4 and 9) — the column is Stage 22's (plan §30 Activate; `crates/human/store` `schema.rs`, migration 9's note), not schema v9's (A 2026-10-25); name_source/photo_source: card | person, with when (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0059 decision 3): a local edit is never overwritten by a card, and the contact page says where a value came from; content-free, in this review
contact_routes(contact_id, local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id, identity_key?, device_label?, verification_note?, last_seen?)   -- a route is one remote user identity held by one local identity (A 2026-10-21 (ii), ADR-0061 decision 4, ADR-0059 decisions 3-4); identity_key: absent until a card is accepted on the route (typed contacts and every route from before ADR-0063 have none), set by the first accepted card, replaced only by the person's accepted key change; the local identities' own private keys live outside this store, one sealed file per identity under the state root; local_endpoint_id is its contact's, carried here so the rule is one index; one contact per (local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id) and at most one route per peer_id under a contact — two endpoints of one PeerId are two contacts of that identity, shown as sharing a node, and another identity's contacts are never consulted; enforced by the store's writers (plan §29 step 2), the two indexes it implies — UNIQUE(local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id) and UNIQUE(contact_id, peer_id) — and FOREIGN KEY(contact_id, local_endpoint_id) REFERENCES contacts(contact_id, local_endpoint_id) ON DELETE CASCADE, backed by UNIQUE(contact_id, local_endpoint_id) on contacts (the parent key SQLite requires), so a route cannot sit under another identity's contact, added in the §Logical tables review, where existing contacts, contact_routes and conversation_index rows are backfilled with the `human` endpoint as their local identity — the column, the composite foreign key and that backfill are Stage 22's (plan §30 Activate; migration 9's note): schema v9 carries `UNIQUE(contact_id, peer_id, endpoint_id)`, `UNIQUE(contact_id, peer_id)` and the single-column foreign key, so a contact holds at most one route per PeerId and one row per route by the schema, while one contact per route is the writers' rule (A 2026-10-25)
conversation_index(conversation_id, local_endpoint_id, peer_id, endpoint_id?, channel_id?, title?, last_activity?, card_shared_at?)   -- local_endpoint_id: the identity the conversation speaks as, for life (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0061 decision 3; the column Stage 22's, as above); card_shared_at: when that identity's card was last shared (ADR-0059 decision 2, A (ii); it replaces last_shared_card, since the card is the identity's); both content-free, in the §Logical tables review
own_cards(endpoint_id, name, surname?, photo?, created_at, updated_at)   -- A 2026-10-21 (ii), ADR-0059 decision 2, ADR-0061 decision 1: one card per user identity, keyed by its EndpointId — the identity's display name and photo, written by the person, outside ADR-0044's three states; the row is the identity's presentation, not its standing (ADR-0061 decision 1: the first identity is the authored human endpoint, every other one an endpoint in the overlay's `added` member); a discard of the store (ADR-0060 decision 6) loses the rows and the next open recreates one per identity with the route label as its name
pending_outbound(local_row_id, conversation_id, app_message_id, transport_message_id,
                 destination_peer?, destination_endpoint?, channel_id?, media_type?, payload,
                 created_at, last_attempt_at?, retry_state)
unread_inbound(local_row_id, conversation_id, app_message_id, source_peer, source_endpoint?,
               channel_id?, media_type?, payload, received_at, kind)
kept_inbound(local_row_id, conversation_id, app_message_id, source_peer, source_endpoint?,
             channel_id?, media_type?, payload, received_at, read_at, kept_at, kind)
settings(key, value)
read_pairs(origin, app_message_id, at)
```

Schema v10 (A 2026-10-27, the start window of `design/2026-10-27-start-window.md`, rust-ui-dev-02's j68; architect-cto's rulings of the same day to the four questions of its first batch) adds two derived, content-free columns and the indexes the start reads need, so that opening the app reads counts and summaries, never every row. `contacts.name_key` is the comparison key of the contact's display name as the store computes it (`store::name_key`: NFKC, case fold, white space, the confusables skeleton — the case key of contact-cards design §5.4.1, the one the store holds today; if the design's other two keys land in the store they are columns beside it under the same version), so that a card's standing against the contacts is a handful of indexed queries rather than every contact decoded per card; it reconstructs nothing the row's `display_name` does not already hold. `kind` on `unread_inbound` and `kept_inbound` is the row's envelope kind as the client's own classification reads it at commit — `text`, `card`, or `other` for a row this build cannot show — so that the unread badge is one indexed count of the decodable kinds and the hidden count the rest, with no row decoded at start; it is RETENTION.md §5's content-free metadata (an index, not content), and `other` names a row, never what it holds. A third rule of the same batch's second half (A 2026-10-27 (ii), rust-ui-dev-02's A1): `conversation_index` holds a row for every direct conversation the store holds rows of — written in the same transaction as the first pending, unread or kept row of that conversation (`INSERT OR IGNORE`), where until then only a shared card wrote one — and an open-time backfill under a `settings` row `conversation_index_version` indexes the direct conversations of every stored pending, unread and kept row once; a peer already past 64 from before keeps what it holds, and only a new endpoint of it is refused. A channel conversation is keyed by its channel and is outside the per-peer bound, which is about a peer's choice of labels. All three are versioned by a `settings` row each (`name_key_version`, `inbound_kind_version`, `conversation_index_version`): a build whose normalisation or classification differs recomputes every key, or reclassifies every row of an older version, at open before any count is read, once per upgrade, bounded by the store's own size. The indexes: `contacts (name_key)` — the same-name standing is asked by the key, and without it every card scans every contact, the cost v10 removes; the contacts table has no `local_endpoint_id` column before Stage 22 (above), so Stage 22's migration, which adds the column and scopes contacts by identity (ADR-0061 decision 4), widens this index to `(local_endpoint_id, name_key)` in the same change — `contact_routes (peer_id, endpoint_id)`, `contact_routes (identity_key)`, the two inbound tables' paging by direct conversation, `(source_peer, source_endpoint_key, received_at, row_id)`, and by channel, `(channel_key, received_at, row_id)` — the two `_key` columns being the tables' generated non-null forms of `source_endpoint` and `channel_id`, which key a conversation; the inbound tables carry no conversation column of their own — and two COVERING indexes for the counts read at start, so that the badge's count per conversation and both hidden counts are answered from an index and read no row (a count that touches a row reads its payload's overflow chain under the cipher, measured 10 to 17 times slower by #489's review): `unread_inbound (kind, source_peer, source_endpoint, channel_id, app_message_id)` — over the table's own columns, not the generated `_key` forms, since SQLite recomputes a virtual column from its row rather than reading the index's copy, which makes the index a search and not a cover (measured by #489's plan test: `USING COVERING INDEX` over the own columns, `USING INDEX` over the `_key` forms); the counts group by `source_peer, source_endpoint` where `channel_id` is null and by `channel_id` otherwise, which is the `_key` grouping for every row the client writes — and `kept_inbound (kind)`, which holds only its hidden count. The unread badge counts the unread rows of a decodable kind in the conversation, one per row (A 2026-10-27 (ii), the owner's second review of the client, judged by the review class at 1b37e0ef): the table's key already scopes a row by its publisher and, for a direct message, its source endpoint, so no two unread rows of one conversation are one message, and two publishers reusing one id on a channel are two rows, two items in the conversation (the model keys a channel item by its publisher, as `read_pairs` below keys a pair) and two in the badge. The sentence first read "distinct `(conversation_id, app_message_id)`", with a kept-item case as its reason; that case the count, read over `unread_inbound` alone, never meets, and the distinct count merged two publishers' rows on a channel while the page showed both, so the store's query and its test changed with this sentence in the start window's second batch (#492, 62d11cb5: both counts `COUNT(*)` over the same covering index, whose trailing `app_message_id` the count no longer needs and v10 keeps), before any screen read the count. Reading an item deletes its unread row as RETENTION.md says. Pending rows keep no count ceiling: a ceiling is a product rule of a later note (the design's §13 Q1), not this fix's. The distinct remote endpoints the store holds per remote PeerId are at most 64 (HUMAN-CHAT.md's malformed rule, A 2026-10-27): the count is the union of `conversation_index`'s rows and `contact_routes`' rows for that peer, so it is durable, bounded by the store, and an endpoint, once indexed, is forgotten by a discard alone (A 2026-10-27 (ii): no operation removes a `conversation_index` row — a conversation is its rows and the index outlives them, and a route's removal forgets nothing — so neither emptying a conversation nor deleting a route resets the bound, and a discard clears all); the transport client checks it before it commits a direct inbound and before a direct send writes its pending row (`crates/human/transport-client`, `admits_endpoint`). The person's own send to a direct endpoint indexes its conversation and counts toward that peer's 64 like an inbound; a send that would open a 65th is refused by the same check before the pending row is written, shown as any refused send is (Not sent, with the reason), its person-facing words ux-designer's if the state needs more than that.

Schema v11 (A 2026-10-27, #492's review F2; built in the start window's second batch with the Chats list's read, rust-ui-dev-02's, and until that batch lands the index is the record's and not yet the build's) widens `kept_inbound_by_kind` from `(kind)` to `(kind, source_peer, source_endpoint, channel_id)` — the shape `unread_inbound_by_kind` has without its trailing `app_message_id`, over the table's own columns for the reason v10 gives — so that the set of conversations the Chats list shows, kept-only ones included (`design/2026-10-27-start-window.md` §3), is read from the two `_by_kind` indexes alone with no kept row read; a conversation's newest row and its time are the first row of the per-conversation paging indexes, not this one's. An index rebuild only: no row is written and no version row is needed, and the plan test that refuses any non-covering step on either inbound table (#492) is what holds it.

`read_pairs` (A 2026-10-03, Stage 15 Q5) is the bounded duplicate-suppression record RETENTION.md §5 allows: a content-free pair naming where a message came from (`origin`: its source peer and endpoint for a direct message, its source peer and channel for a broadcast — the store's own inbound identity, so two peers reusing one id on a channel are two pairs, never one) and the application id the sender gave it, with the time the pair was written — nothing else, pinned by the store's shape guard. It is written when a row is marked read and when Keep is removed, so a copy of a message that was read and not kept, re-sent after a restart, does not reappear as unread; a copy carrying the same id with different text is suppressed too, because the id is the sender's claim that it is the same message and a content-free table must take it at its word. At most 4096 rows; the oldest is evicted inside the transaction that inserts the newest. It joins the owner's privacy review beside `conversation_index` and `contact_routes.last_seen`.

A current-session RAM model may also hold transport-terminal outbound messages and read-but-unkept inbound messages for display. Those rows are not serialized as general message history.

No transport private key, trust allowlist, endpoint lease, Kademlia bucket, relay reservation, AutoNAT evidence, transport direct-message dedup record (ADR-0019 — `read_pairs` above is the application's, keyed on the application id), or endpoint-directory cache is stored here.

## Retention transitions

- user sends -> durable `pending_outbound` is committed **before** the first transport call;
- direct `AcceptedV2` -> delete durable pending content;
- successful broadcast publication -> delete durable pending content, without claiming recipient delivery;
- inbound event consumed -> durable `unread_inbound` is committed before normal notification/UI presentation;
- inbound becomes locally read -> delete durable unread content unless the receiver explicitly chooses `Keep`;
- receiver chooses `Keep` after read -> durable `kept_inbound`;
- receiver removes `Keep` -> delete durable kept content.

Remote data can never set the Keep state.

## Message-status honesty

Application state may record:

- local send requested / pending;
- transport accepted remotely for direct;
- broadcast locally published;
- transport rejected/timeout;
- local inbound received;
- local UI read state;
- local receiver Keep state.

It must not label transport acceptance as “read by human” or “processed by app” without a future application-level receipt protocol. Local `read`/`Keep` are not sent remotely in v1.

## Where the store lives

The database and the single-instance lock beside it sit in the human client's own directory under the profile's state root — `ProfilePaths::human_dir()`, `<state_dir>/human` (A 2026-10-03, Stage 15 Q3) — never among the daemon's files (its sockets, profile lock and identity key), and the profile's `identity.key_file` may not point inside it — judged on disk, links followed (`configuration.md`; the lexical check landed with Stage 15's batch 2, the on-disk one A 2026-10-05). Deleting the directory loses only what RETENTION.md allows to be lost. One client file outside it is the sealed store key, `<state_dir>/human-store.key` (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0060 decision 3): kept out of `human/` so that an export or a copy of the store's directory leaves the key behind, and kept out of the daemon's identity directory because the daemon's secrets are not the client's; deleting `human/` leaves it, harmless and useless alone, and recreating the store replaces it. The others are the identities' sealed key files under `<state_dir>/identity-keys/` (ADR-0063 decision 2; A 2026-10-21): outside `human/` for the same reasons, and unlike the store key they survive a discard and recreate of the store — every identity keeps its key and fingerprint; Remove of the identity deletes its file, and a Keystore invalidation leaves it unusable until the person creates a new key for the identity (ADR-0063 decision 2).

## Store health

Unread durability is a human-client property, not a transport guarantee. If SQLite is unavailable/full/corrupt such that new unread content cannot be committed, the human client exposes degraded storage health, releases/disables its direct EndpointId, and suspends its local human broadcast joins/delivery until storage is healthy rather than present itself as a normally durable receiver. Profile `channels.desired` may remain network-prewarmed without local delivery.

There remains a bounded handoff window after transport endpoint-queue acceptance and before human-store commit. `AcceptedV2` is not redefined.

## Migrations

Every schema migration is transactional and versioned. Migration failure puts the human app database into recovery/read-only/export mode; it never triggers transport identity regeneration. Stage 15's desktop client ships the recovery half (A 2026-10-03, Q4): a blocking screen with no session and no lease, showing the database path, offering Quit and Retry, and never renaming, moving or deleting the file; the read-only open and the export are carried by name (plan §18). One step is file-level, by exception (A 2026-10-21, ADR-0060 decision 6): the rewrite of a plaintext `human.sqlite` into an encrypted one — seal and sync the store key, export to `human.sqlite.encrypting`, rename it to `human.sqlite.encrypted` as the durable mark of a complete copy, scrub the plaintext in place while it holds its name, unlink its `-wal`, `-shm` and then the file, rename the encrypted file into place, each with a directory sync — with its crash states told apart by name on the next open: plaintext beside `.encrypting` restarts the rewrite from the intact plaintext, plaintext beside `.encrypted` keeps the copy and resumes the scrub, `.encrypted` alone finishes the rename, a `human.sqlite` that opens under the key is the store; the platform reporting the key invalidated offers discard-and-recreate directly; anything else — a present key that does not open the file, no key file, an unlisted combination of names — goes to recovery mode, in that order. From this date the recovery screen offers a third, confirmed action, **Discard and recreate**, beside Quit and Retry — the one relaxation of its never-delete rule, on the person's word only — because on the desktop a lost key file and a damaged never-migrated store leave the same files and the screen must not trap the person. The recovery screen's never-rename rule is about a store that failed to migrate, and does not apply to this rewrite.

A migration from any prototype/general-history schema must not preserve full history by default. It must explicitly classify rows into pending-outbound, unread-inbound, receiver-kept-inbound, or discard message content according to ADR-0044.

## Backup policy

Android system backup/device transfer excludes the entire human-store database, its sealed store key and the rest of the application's data, permanently (ADR-0060 decision 8, the owner's word of 2026-10-21): InterWeave data never leaves the device through a platform backup, a platform transfer or any external provider; the backup that will exist travels over InterWeave itself between the person's own devices, by a later record; until then a new device starts from the recovery phrase and an empty store.

A future explicit encrypted application backup may include **message content only from `unread_inbound` and `kept_inbound`**. `pending_outbound` is local crash/restart state but is excluded from portable backup to prevent restored/second devices from becoming implicit replay/delayed-send sources.

Contacts/configuration backup remains a separate policy and does not change message-retention eligibility.
