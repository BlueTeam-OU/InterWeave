# The automated client — what the transport promises it, and what it owes itself

**Status:** architecture/design, bound to ADR-0055 (Accepted 2026-10-19 by the arming of the pull request that landed both; plan §28 names it). An automated client is any long-lived machine process that is a consumer of a profile's daemon — an agent, a bot, a gateway, a test harness. It stands beside the human client (`../human/`) and the Claude Code Channel bridge (`../../plugin/`) with the same standing and under the same transport rules; nothing here narrows the human client, and no coordination protocol is named here, because the transport is general purpose and a deployment's protocol is the deployment's.

Every fact below names the case that holds it in `tests/automated-client/tests/automated_client.rs` or the contract that states it. A constant is cited, never restated: the suite reads the ingress bucket's constants from the runtime crate, and the ceilings from the contracts.

## 1. What the client is

One process per profile, running under the profile's account, holding the data-socket lease of one configured endpoint under one client kind the deployment chooses (`endpoints.entries`, `allowed_client_kinds`), negotiating keepalive, reconnecting with bounded backoff, a fresh lease and fresh joins when the daemon goes away and comes back, and draining its event queue as part of holding the lease (LOCAL-IPC.md §Hello, §Keepalive, §Message-event routing; the bridge's shape, ADR-0002 A 2026-10-07). It never opens the admin socket: trust changes stay with `transportctl` under the account's own hand (ADR-0037).

## 2. What the transport promises

| Promise | Held by |
|---|---|
| A direct message reaches the receiver over a loopback static-bootstrap entry as the only configured route (operator-supplied, ADR-0052 rule 9). | `a_direct_message_lands_over_a_static_loopback_entry` |
| `AcceptedV2` is queue admission at the receiver, never delivery (ADR-0044; LOCAL-CLIENT.md §4). | `a_withheld_lease_is_retried_under_one_id_and_lands_once` (a retry inside the window is accepted again and not delivered again); the receipt case below shows what delivery takes |
| `no_route` is coarse (ADR-0005): unknown, offline, disabled and denied are one answer. | contract; held on the wire by `tests/security/tests/endpoint_probing.rs`'s `the_four_no_route_causes_are_byte_identical_on_the_wire` |
| There is no durable broadcast and no offline mailbox (ADR-0020). A publish reaches the peers subscribed at that moment. | contract (ADR-0020); the bucket case below shows only that a dropped publish did not arrive within its three-second watch |
| Duplicate suppression is bounded and in memory (ADR-0019: 10,000 entries, five minutes, persistence prohibited). A retry under one `message_id` lands once within the receiver's window and uptime; after the window, or after the receiving daemon restarts, it is a second delivery. | `a_withheld_lease_is_retried_under_one_id_and_lands_once` (inside the window: accepted again, not delivered again; after B's restart: delivered again, collapsed by B's client) |
| The per-client queue holds 256 events; a direct message that finds it full is refused `overloaded` before `AcceptedV2`; a broadcast drops the oldest; a client that does not drain is closed as wedged and loses its lease. | LOCAL-IPC.md (the 256, and the lease lost with a wedged close); the full-queue refusal is held by the runtime's own case `a_full_queue_refuses_rather_than_accepting_falsely` (`crates/transport/runtime/src/direct_inbound.rs`, at bound 1), a broadcast dropping the oldest by `crates/transport/runtime/src/session_queue.rs`'s `a_full_queue_drops_for_that_session_and_nobody_else`, and the wedged close by `crates/local/ipc-server/src/server.rs`'s `a_silent_client_that_stopped_reading_is_closed_and_frees_its_slot`. The direct-burst case below drains the receiver after every send, so its refusals come from the receiver's per-peer bucket, not from a full queue. |
| The receiver's broadcast ingress bucket (the runtime's constants: 32 in a burst, 120 a minute per peer) drops what exceeds it before local delivery, with no refusal to the sender, no log line and no counter. Measured 2026-10-19 over thirteen runs: 34–41 of 200 delivered, each run inside the bound the bucket's constants give over its elapsed time (the case computes it; a later run printed 50 within a bound of 31–56 over 11.9 s). | `a_burst_of_200_broadcasts_is_bounded_by_the_ingress_bucket` |
| The same burst as direct sends lands whole: 200 of 200, each once. The refusal count is a sample that depends on timing — the client's 100 ms retry step and the round trip against the bucket's 0.5 s refill — not a promise: 632–686 `Overloaded` refusals over thirteen runs, 568 in a later one; the duration follows from 168 refills at two a second, about 84 s — 32 at once, then two a second to one peer. | `the_same_burst_as_direct_sends_lands_whole_through_overloaded_retries` |
| **Broadcast order is not preserved.** One run in thirteen delivered out of the publisher's order; the case reports whether order held and asserts nothing about it. Direct sends from one sender, one at a time and retried in turn, arrived in order in every run, and nothing promises that either. | the two burst cases |
| The payload ceiling is 48 KiB (ADR-0026, TRANSPORT.md) and does not rise for any client; the IPC frame is 128 KiB. | contract; held by `crates/api/ipc-protocol/src/request.rs`'s `a_payload_past_the_ceiling_is_payload_too_large` |
| A clean daemon stop answers `ShuttingDown` on the session and every later call; a vanished daemon answers `BackendUnavailable`. Both mean the session is gone and is re-opened: one fresh lease and join, and the next message lands. | `a_daemon_gone_under_a_live_client_is_reopened_either_way` (SIGTERM, then SIGKILL) |
| An application-level receipt, sent by the receiving client after it has kept the message, reaches the sender as an ordinary direct message. The transport carries it opaquely and knows nothing of it (ADR-0001). | `an_application_receipt_reaches_the_sender_after_the_receiver_kept_it` |
| A fan-out under one `message_id` to several receivers lands once at each; a second fan-out under the same id is suppressed per destination. | `a_fan_out_under_one_id_lands_once_at_each_receiver` |
| Two daemons of one account share a port silently (`SO_REUSEPORT`; two `LISTEN` sockets in the kernel's table). A port held by a socket without `SO_REUSEPORT` makes the daemon exit 1 on `EADDRINUSE` (`a_port_held_without_reuseport_refuses_the_start`, with a free-port start as its control; that any listen failure exits 1 is held by `tests/desktop-e2e/tests/daemon.rs`'s unbindable-address case), and its unit retries it (the unit's retry measured on the host's user manager, plan §28 step 4, not a case of this suite). That another account is refused the port rests on the kernel's documented rule and is not measured here (one uid in the suite). | `two_daemons_of_one_account_share_a_port`; the unit's retry measured on the host's user manager (plan §28 step 4) |

## 3. What a client that needs more owes itself

Above the socket, as every client states its own guarantee (ADR-0044 made the human client do so):

- its own durable pending record, one `message_id` kept per message for retry, and a retry never given up on without its operator — the human client's schedule (1 s doubling to a 5 min cap, unbounded in count) is a known-good shape;
- its own receipt where it needs delivery rather than admission (§2's receipt row), and its own collapse of a repeated message by its own identity, since the transport's window is bounded;
- fan-out as one direct send per recipient when every recipient must receive, since a publish reaches only the subscribed, the bucket drops silently and order is not kept; paced at what §2 measured;
- compression and chunking under its own framing when its messages exceed the ceiling (`;ce=br` is the content-encoding the human facade defines, HUMAN-CHAT.md);
- its own authentication of who a PeerId is: the transport authenticates the PeerId and nothing above it (ADR-0012: a PeerId is not a person, a role or a principal).

## 4. The one-host deployment

Each profile's `config.yaml`, rendered by the deployment and never hand-edited on the host (the suite's `Profile::render` is the shape): `trust.policy: static-allowlist` with every other profile's PeerId; one `endpoints.entries` row admitting one client kind; one `static-bootstrap` provider listing the others as `/ip4/127.0.0.1/tcp/<port>/p2p/<PeerId>`; `transport.listen.addresses` on `127.0.0.1` only; nothing else. A port per profile, fixed by the deployment, one profile per account. Identity without a daemon: `transportctl identity init --profile <name>` mints the key and prints the PeerId, `identity show` prints it for an existing key, both under the profile lock (ADR-0055 decision 4). Service units: `packaging/linux/systemd/interweave-daemon@.service` and `interweave-client@.service`, templated on the profile name, the client `After=` and `BindsTo=` its daemon, running the account's own binaries; the client unit runs the deployment's program from the account's `libexec` or a drop-in's `ExecStart`, and InterWeave ships none.

## 5. Not promised, not measured

A second host (ADR-0034 §7 and SPIKE-004's limits); the cross-account port refusal (§2); any ordering across senders; Byzantine resistance among profiles; protection from same-uid code.
