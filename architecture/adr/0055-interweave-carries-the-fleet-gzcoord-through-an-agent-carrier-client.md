# InterWeave carries the fleet's GZCoord through an agent carrier client that owns durability, under a committed PeerId roster

**Status:** Proposed — awaiting the owner's acceptance, given by arming the pull request that lands this record (the ratification-by-merge rule of 2026-09-27)

## Context

The agents that build InterWeave talk over GZCOORD/1, carried today by a central HTTP relay on the one host: one process, one shared bearer token, a `sender` field that is a claim, pull delivery through each session's watch, and one database that is the channel's only record (agent-fabric ADR-033, which names "InterWeave's cross-host transport" as what comes next and states that nothing integrates it). The owner's direction of 2026-10-19 is that the fleet starts to use InterWeave instead.

What InterWeave already is, read from `main` (441361da) on 2026-10-19: a profile-scoped daemon with a PeerId per profile (ADR-0015, ADR-0016); directed delivery to one endpoint under a PeerId with `AcceptedV2` as bounded queue admission and a coarse `no_route` (ADR-0005, ADR-0030, ADR-0018); signed GossipSub broadcast whose publisher is the authenticated PeerId (ADR-0004, ADR-0029); a deny-by-default trust allowlist with a persisted overlay (ADR-0012, ADR-0028); IPC v2 over a per-uid socket with an exclusive endpoint lease, a 48 KiB payload and a 128 KiB frame (ADR-0017, ADR-0026, ADR-0037); static bootstrap whose operator-supplied addresses are admitted whatever their class, loopback included (ADR-0052 rule 9); and a transport that is realtime and non-durable by decision (ADR-0018, ADR-0020, ADR-0044: no offline mailbox, durability only inside a client). What it is not: a profile-creation or identity-show command (a desktop `config.yaml` is hand-written; the key is minted by the daemon's `--create-identity`), a service unit (`packaging/linux` is a stub; Stage 19's), or any document that names an automated, non-human client as a first-party consumer.

The fleet is about thirty Linux logins on one host, each with its own `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, its own account-held secrets (agent-fabric ADR-038) and its own episodic journal of the GZCoord messages it sent and was addressed (agent-fabric ADR-041). The relay's control plane (agent-fabric ADR-029) rides the same relay on a second channel.

## Decision

1. **The agent carrier is a first-party local client**, beside the human client and the Claude Code Channel bridge: one long-lived process per login, running under that login, holding the data-socket lease of the endpoint `gzcoord` with the client kind `gzcoord-carrier`, joined to the ChannelId `gzapp:gzcoord`, and reconnecting to its daemon with bounded backoff, a fresh lease and fresh joins when the daemon goes away and comes back — the bridge's shape (ADR-0002 A 2026-10-07, pull delivery). The profile has one `endpoints.entries` row for it, `allowed_client_kinds: [gzcoord-carrier]`, and it is the only client of that endpoint.
2. **The mapping is total and the transport adds no field** (agent-fabric ADR-033 rule 1): `TO: <host>/<login>` is `direct.send` to that login's PeerId at endpoint `gzcoord`; `TO-ROLE` and `BROADCAST` are `broadcast.publish` on `gzapp:gzcoord`, the addressee filter staying the receiver's as today; `IN-REPLY-TO` stays the protocol's correlation and no reply token is used. The GZCOORD/1 text is the payload, `media_type` `text/plain; charset=utf-8`, within the 48 KiB ceiling — a message past it is refused by the carrier before any send, and the notification-cut rule of the inbox is unchanged.
3. **Who a login is on the wire is a committed roster, never the transport's claim.** A roster file in agent-fabric maps each `<host>/<login>` to its profile's PeerId (public), its listen address on the host and nothing else; it is generated at provisioning from each login's own key, and it is the source of every profile's `trust.allowed_peers` and static-bootstrap entries. The carrier refuses an inbound whose authenticated `source_peer` is not the roster's PeerId for the message's `FROM`, and refuses to send a `FROM` that is not its own login's. That is the transport-native sender identity the adapter contract asks for, and it closes the relay's "any token holder can post as any address": a login's key is in its own account and nowhere else.
4. **Durability belongs to the carrier, and the transport stays what ADR-0018, ADR-0020 and ADR-0044 decided.** Outbound: the carrier writes the message to the login's journal as pending before the first attempt, sends with one 128-bit `message_id` kept with the row, retries on `RemoteEndpointUnavailable`, `PeerUnreachable`, `PeerUnknown`, `Overloaded` and `Timeout` with the human client's schedule (1 s doubling to a 5 min cap, unbounded in count), marks the row terminal on `AcceptedV2` or a successful publish, and never gives a message up on its own; a refusal that no retry mends (`UnauthorizedPeer`, `InvalidArgument`) is the sender's to see. Inbound: the carrier appends every `message.direct` and `message.broadcast` event to the journal before it is offered to the inbox, so a session that is absent reads later from its own journal, not from a central log; the window between the daemon's enqueue and the journal's append is the same loss window the human client states, and is stated, not closed. Moving a spool into the runtime stays rejected (ADR-0044 §Alternatives).
5. **Delivery into a session stays pull.** The inbox watch reads the login's journal and cursor exactly as it reads the relay's today (agent-fabric ADR-022 is untouched); pushing a message into a Claude Code session through the Channel bridge is an option the fabric may take later, under its own decision, and is not required for the carrier.
6. **The first deployment is one host over loopback**, each profile listening on `127.0.0.1` with a port of its own from the roster, every other login's PeerId allowed and its loopback address a static-bootstrap entry (operator-supplied, admitted under ADR-0052 rule 9). No connectivity behaviour is needed for it: the server roles stay off, the client roles that every daemon profile composes have no static servers to use, and ADR-0034 §7's release gate stays where it is. A second host is the day ADR-0034 §7 and SPIKE-004's deferred limits are consumed, not before, and ADR-0035's path rules then apply unchanged.
7. **Provisioning is declarative and the transport gains two offline commands.** `transportctl identity init --profile <name>` mints a key with no daemon and prints the PeerId; `transportctl identity show --profile <name>` prints it for an existing key; both take the profile lock as the other identity verbs do. The profile's `config.yaml` is rendered from the roster by the fabric's bootstrap, never hand-edited on a login, and a trust change reaches a running daemon through `transportctl trust allow|revoke` (the persisted overlay) until the next render.
8. **The relay is kept as the fallback and the control plane's carrier during the soak.** Phase 1 carries GZCoord traffic only, the relay still running and the human relay still the last fallback; phase 2 moves `fabric:control` onto a second ChannelId when agent-fabric ADR-029's holder decides it; the relay is retired only after both, on the fabric's record. Nothing here retires it.
9. **The validation bar is agent-fabric ADR-033 rule 4 and the candidate record's "not verified" list, in InterWeave's own tests first**: two profiles on one host, B receiving A's direct and A's broadcast through the carrier's operations, the retry under a withheld lease landing once the lease returns with no duplicate, a daemon restart under a live carrier, and the behaviour under a burst — before any login's watch is pointed at it.

## Alternatives considered

A spool inside `TransportRuntime` (rejected by ADR-0044 and again here: the transport's guarantee stays realtime so that every client states its own durability). The Claude Code Channel bridge as the carrier process itself (it has no standalone mode, a bounded in-memory queue, no durable pending and reply tokens that die with it; its pull mode may serve as the carrier's IPC half, which is the builder's choice, not a rule). A central InterWeave profile that every login talks to over IPC (one key for thirty logins: the relay's authentication gap again, and against ADR-038's one-credential-per-login). Relay, AutoNAT and DCUtR for the first deployment (nothing to traverse on one host; the release gate is not cleared). Hand-written `config.yaml` per login (thirty copies that drift; the roster renders them). Retiring the relay with the first cut-over (no fallback during the period the grammar's freeze is first exercised by an automated transport).

## Consequences

Every login gets an authenticated wire identity and its own credential; the fleet's record of its traffic becomes thirty journals instead of one database, and a fleet-wide history is a question for the fabric (agent-fabric ADR-041's backfill), not for the transport. A message to a login whose daemon or carrier is down is retried by the sender's carrier until it lands, so "offline" costs latency, not loss, as long as the sender's carrier lives; a session reads what arrived while it was absent from its journal. Ordering is per sender, not global. The carrier and the daemon are two more processes per login, supervised by the login's user manager.

## Security implications

The trust boundary moves from one shared token to thirty keys, each in its own account, and the transport authenticates the publisher of every message (ADR-0029 for broadcast, the Noise-authenticated PeerId for direct); the roster is public data and grants nothing by itself — trust is each profile's allowlist, rendered from it. What this record does not claim: Byzantine resistance among logins (a login that is compromised speaks as itself, which is what the roster makes visible), protection from same-uid code, or any property of a second host. The per-uid socket keeps one login's carrier out of another's daemon. The carrier is a client, not an administrator: it never opens the admin socket, and trust changes stay with `transportctl` under the account's own hand.

## Operational implications

Two systemd user units per login (the daemon, the carrier), installed by the fabric's bootstrap from InterWeave's `packaging/linux`; the roster regenerated and every `config.yaml` re-rendered when a login is added, renamed or retired, with a daemon restart or an overlay change; thirty loopback ports from one allocation; the relay kept running through the soak; a runbook for "a login cannot be reached" that reads the sender's journal first (pending rows, their last problem) and the receiver's daemon second.

## Implementation implications

In this repository, a stage that runs beside Stage 17 by record (plan §28): the two `transportctl` verbs, the Linux user units, a conformance suite under `tests/fleet-carrier` proving decision 9 against two daemons on loopback, and `architecture/clients/agent/` stating what the transport promises an automated client. In agent-fabric, by its own records: the carrier process (Python over IPC v2's active contracts, or driving the bridge's pull mode — the builder's choice), the roster and its rendering in `bootstrap.sh`, the inbox and send reading and writing the journal instead of the relay, agent-fabric ADR-033's successor and ADR-029's phase 2. The lane line is the socket: InterWeave ends at the data socket's contract; the carrier and everything above it is the fabric's.

## Revisit conditions

A second host (ADR-0034 §7 cleared, SPIKE-004's limits consumed); a fleet-wide ordered record becoming a requirement again (then a durable relay-like service is a new decision, not a runtime spool); the Channel bridge gaining a standalone mode; the payload ceiling proving too small for a message class the fleet needs (then split at the protocol, never raise the ceiling here).
