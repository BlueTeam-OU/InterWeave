# ADR-0050 — amendment history

### Amendment 2026-08-17 — Markdown dialect pinned to CommonMark 0.31.2 with two named GFM extensions

Rule 2 promised an exact grammar and then named "CommonMark, plus the table and strikethrough extensions". CommonMark is versioned, and tables and strikethrough are not part of it — they are extensions whose grammars differ between implementations. Clients could therefore disagree about whether a given source is a table, a strikethrough, a link destination, or literal text, and that disagreement occurs before the security and dimension rules can be applied to the parse.

The rule now pins CommonMark 0.31.2 and the `table` and `strikethrough` grammars of GFM 0.29-gfm specifically, and states that no other GFM extension is in the subset.

### Amendment 2026-08-17 — Raw envelopes over the decompressed ceiling are too large before compression

Rule 4 permitted compression whenever the raw envelope exceeded `max_payload_bytes`, with no upper bound on the raw size, while rule 5 required every receiver to abort decoding past 196,608 bytes. A highly compressible raw envelope above that ceiling therefore satisfied the send rule and could not be accepted by any conforming receiver.

Rule 4 now classifies such an envelope as too large before compression is considered, making the sender's legal compression range `max_payload_bytes < raw <= 196,608`. Compressibility does not extend the ceiling; the prior wording implied it could.

### Amendment 2026-08-17 — Bridge decodes content-encoding before classifying content

Rule 6 required the Claude bridge's defense-in-depth checks to run on decompressed bytes, but `contracts/CHANNEL-EVENT.md` independently required non-UTF-8 payloads to be forwarded as base64url and stated that the bridge "does not parse JSON/application protocols to infer meaning". A brotli-compressed envelope is non-UTF-8, so a bridge conforming to that contract would forward opaque base64url and never invoke the decoder, while a bridge following rule 6 would violate the event contract. Two accepted documents in conflict is precisely what must not be resolved silently in code (`CLAUDE.md` §2).

`CHANNEL-EVENT.md` now states that a content-encoding parameter is decoded before content classification, and that decoding is representation rather than the application-protocol parsing it forbids — the bridge still reads no envelope field to infer meaning. `meta.content_type` reports the media type with the encoding parameter removed. Rule 6 cites that rule rather than restating it.

### Amendment 2026-08-21 — Decoding aborts when the output would exceed the ceiling, not when it reaches it

Rule 4 gives the sender the inclusive range `max_payload_bytes < raw <= 196,608`, while rule 5 required the receiver to abort "when it is reached". An envelope of exactly 196,608 raw bytes was therefore sender-conforming and refused by every conforming receiver — the identical sender-conforming/universally-unacceptable gap the 2026-08-17 amendment closed at the other end of the range, reopened at one value by the boundary wording.

There was no coherent behaviour to implement: the two rules disagreed about a single size. `crates/human/chat-protocol` had already resolved it in rule 4's favour — its decoder accepts exactly the ceiling and refuses the next byte — so the prose was the part that was wrong.

Rule 5 now aborts as soon as the output *would exceed* the cap. 196,608 bytes decode; the 196,609th aborts. The ceiling stays 4 × the 49,152-byte transport payload ceiling, and that transport ceiling is inclusive too, so the multiplier now means the same thing at both ends.

### Amendment 2026-10-01 — Subset validation is the human clients'; the bridge decodes only

Rule 6 said one shared library implements decode-with-cap and subset validation for the desktop client, the Android client, and the Claude bridge. `contracts/CHANNEL-EVENT.md` says the bridge decodes a content-encoding and does not parse the envelope — it reads no `text`, no `reply_to` — and subset validation is parsing: it needs the CommonMark parser to find a nesting level, a table dimension or a link destination. The two could not both hold, and the contract governs the bridge's behaviour.

Rule 6 now gives decode-with-cap to all three consumers and subset validation to the two human clients, and says the bridge leaves the validator off. Plan §17 (3) pins the shape: the parser sits behind an off-by-default `markdown` feature of `crates/human/chat-protocol`, so the bridge's default-feature dependency graph names no parser. The review of the Stage 14 record found the conflict (architect-cto's review class, 2026-10-01); `CHANNEL-EVENT.md`'s "size and subset checks below" is corrected in the same change to name only the decoded-size cap it enforces.

### Amendment 2026-10-03 — The envelope schema is `active`

The acceptance paragraph said `contracts/schemas/human-chat/envelope` was `approved` because nothing implemented it. Stage 14 implemented it: `crates/human/chat-protocol` parses and emits the envelope, `tests/human-chat/tests/envelope_schema.rs` binds the schema and the parser to one verdict over the 23 frozen vectors and validates what the crate emits, and `tests/desktop-e2e/tests/human_chat.rs` validates every payload two daemons handed their clients — direct and broadcast, plain and `;ce=br` — against the schema. The Stage 14 close (plan §17's closing record) flipped the schema and its manifest to `active` under ADR-0049, on the owner's word.

The paragraph now says the status the schema had and when it changed. Nothing else in the record moves: the status of a contract is ADR-0049's to govern, and this note exists so the acceptance paragraph does not read as a current claim.

### Amendment 2026-11-04 — What a human client draws of one body unasked is bounded

Rule 2 bounded nesting depth and table dimensions and required render cost to stay linear, and the specification's 2026-10-01 amendment added the inline bound on the ground that "a peer's message must never" abort the process. Nothing bounded how tall one drawn body could be. rust-ui-dev measured on 2026-11-04 that the desktop client's renderer keeps item geometry in 16-bit pixels and panics once one drawn item passes 32,767 of them: a valid message of between 1,350 and 1,500 one-letter list items, 16 KB of source and well under every size ceiling, panicked the draw at scale 1, and a single paragraph of 200 KB — a renderer probe past the message ceiling, not a message a conforming peer can send — did the same. The plain-text fallback did not avoid it, the source being as tall. So any peer the person trusted could stop the desktop client with one ordinary message, which is the shape the inline-nesting amendment ruled out for the parser, in the renderer.

Two shapes were weighed. Drawing a body in bounded pieces fixes the panic alone and leaves a body of some 49,000 lines, at the ceiling, between the person and the next message, more than a thousand screens on a phone. Cutting the body in the conversation and reading it whole in a view the person opens fixes both, and is what the design chose (`design/2026-11-04-long-message.md`). The bound is the contract's and not a renderer's, for the reason the nesting bound is: what a peer's message can make a client do is the contract's to limit, and a figure left to each renderer would let the clients disagree about what a person sees of the same message.

Rule 2 now bounds what a human client draws of one body unasked, beside nesting and tables. The figures are the specification's: a body of more than 2,000 grapheme clusters or more than 40 model lines is cut to a preview of at most 1,600 and 32, the fallback and Show source cut by the same rule over the source's lines, the whole body read in a reader on the person's press, the whole source there and copied out in one action, so the source stays always available. The condition any other figures must keep is written beside them: every whole body at the bound, in its tallest shape, on the narrowest layout, at the highest display scale either client supports, draws under the renderer's ceiling; the design's first figures, 2,400 and 48, were estimated to fail it for a body of headings at 200 % text and were lowered before this amendment was written. An earlier ruling of the same day, a cap on drawn line boxes with the source paged, is withdrawn by this one. The cut is a drawing rule only: it rejects no envelope and changes no read, Keep or retention state, and the bridge forwards the whole text. The matrix row and the tests that prove it land with rust-ui-dev's build.

### Amendment 2026-11-04 (ii) — The drawn-body count is of what is drawn, each time it is drawn

The amendment above counted the grapheme clusters over the envelope's `text` as sent, markup and link destinations included, so that nothing drawn would be outside the count. ux-designer found on the same day, from #561's review, that it was not so: several constructs draw more than their source. An autolink's address is drawn as the line's text and again as its control's label; every control's label is the client's words plus the destination made visible, which spells a hidden code point as its code, so one link padded with some 60,000 variation selectors was a handful of clusters in the source and a label of some 480,000 characters; a reference link's destination is in the source once and drawn once per use; and `![]()` is five characters and draws a placeholder of seventeen. Under the rule as written a body under both figures could be drawn whole and still put one item past the renderer's ceiling, and the reader did not help, since a control is one item and its label was drawn whole there too. None of it was drawn or measured; the heights were estimates from the design's Pixel 9a figures.

Rule 2 now says what the count is of: what the client draws of the body, each time it draws it — the drawn text, every link control's label as shown, once per link however the source spells the destination, and every image placeholder as shown. The count is the client's own drawing and not the source, because the ceiling is on what is drawn and the source is a poor proxy for it, as the four shapes show. And every drawn item is within the same figures wherever it is drawn, a link control's label never cut: a link whose control's label, as shown, would pass the whole-body cluster figure gets no control, its text drawn inert as a link of a scheme outside the allow-list is, the whole destination staying in the source. The first draft of this amendment cut such a label at the figure with a line saying it continued; the review found that a cut label shows one host and opens another — a destination padded with user-info before the `@` — which is the spoof the whole label was there to defeat, so the draw-nothing rule replaced the cut. The reader draws in pieces each within the whole-body figures, so the condition the figures must keep covers a piece as it covers a whole body. The plan's §18 ruling (15), which has each link's control name its full destination, now says that a link past the figure has no control at all. The tests that prove the four shapes land with rust-ui-dev's build, under the design's A3 and A4.

### Amendment 2026-11-05 (ii) — A line is a drawn line, and no drawn item spans more than 8,000 logical pixels

The 2026-11-04 amendment made the specification's figures stand in for the desktop renderer's ceiling in two ways, and rust-ui-dev measured both failing on 2026-11-05 while building the cut. A line was "one of the parse's ... however it wraps", so a paragraph of 1,000 hard line breaks in 2,000 characters was one line and whole by the count, and drew a thousand lines in one bubble. And the condition for the figures was "every whole body at the bound ... at the highest display scale either client supports, draws under that ceiling", but the scale is the platform's: a body of 40 first-level headings at 200 % text, 360 dp wide, measured 22,286 physical pixels at scale 2 and 33,429 at scale 3, past the 32,767 ceiling, and a desktop at 300 % is ordinary on a dense screen. Lowering the figures for each scale was a race no figure wins, and naming a highest supported scale would have been a limit the client cannot enforce without blurring the text.

Rule 2 now says what a line is and separates the panic from the figures. A line is a drawn line: the parse's plus one per hard line break inside it; soft wrapping still counts nothing, since the count may depend on neither width nor scale. A hard line break is defined by what the client draws and not by the grammar alone: CommonMark's hard line break, and every mandatory break of UAX #14 — LF, CR, CRLF, VT, FF, NEL, U+2028 and U+2029 — in the drawn text, while a CommonMark soft line break, drawn as a space, counts nothing. The first text of this amendment left the term undefined, and ux-designer-01's review found (#564 round 7) that read as CommonMark's alone it missed the breaks the client's layout makes: CommonMark keeps U+2028, VT, FF and NEL as paragraph text, so a paragraph of 1,000 U+2028 in 2,000 characters would have been one line and whole by the count, and drawn a thousand — the case the amendment was written to close. The figures stay 2,000 and 40 for the person and the first draw's cost. What keeps the renderer from panicking is a per-item invariant: no drawn item spans more than 8,000 logical pixels in width or height, the bubble's fill, the row surface, the swipe reveal, the focus ring and a selection drawn in pieces per drawn line or per block, so that no item's geometry depends on the body's extent; at 8,000 a scale of 4 is 32,000 physical, under the ceiling, and the client never clamps the platform's scale. Width is in the invariant because an unwrapped code line of 2,000 clusters is wider than the ceiling at scale 2. The measured condition became per item: at 200 % text, on the narrowest layout, at the highest scale the probe can set, every drawn item of a body at the bound in each of the design's shapes is within 8,000 logical pixels in both dimensions and the body draws without a panic; a failure makes the pieces smaller, never the text, and never raises the figures. The specification's Bounds bullet carries the rule, with the design's §6 and A4 its derivation and measurement (rust-ui-dev's observations 01a1aabf-1504 and 01a1ab0f-c665).

### Amendment 2026-11-05 — A drawn grapheme cluster has at most 32 code points

rust-ui-dev measured on 2026-11-05 that the desktop client's shaper counts a cluster's shaping characters in an unsigned 8-bit integer and adds to it unguarded, and that the release profile turns the overflow into a stop: one grapheme cluster of 256 code points — a letter and 255 U+0301, 511 bytes — received as a body stopped the client wherever it was drawn; the same text with 254 marks drew, in the same run. No bound on a body's size covers it, because the bound is per cluster and a body of any size can carry one, and the client stays stopped for as long as the message draws on start — an unread one does. It is the class the nesting bound closed for the parser: a trusted peer's half-kilobyte message must never stop the client, so the bound is the contract's and not the renderer's, whatever the renderer later does about its counter.

Rule 2 now bounds the cluster beside nesting and tables: no drawn extended grapheme cluster (UAX #29, the unit the clients already segment by) has more than 32 code points. The figure follows what conforming text needs rather than the counter's size: UAX #15's Stream-Safe Text Format bounds conforming text at 30 non-starters after a starter, 31 code points in one cluster, and the longest RGI emoji ZWJ sequence is 10, so 32 admits every cluster a conforming producer sends and leaves an 8-bit counter at an eighth of its range; a figure set at the counter less a margin would not survive a renderer with a smaller counter or a wider cluster rule. Of the two buildable shapes — draw the first code points of the cluster and drop the rest of its marks, or draw the whole cluster as one replacement character — the second is the rule: the specification already forbids a cut inside a cluster, a partly drawn cluster is exactly that, and marks dropped silently alter what was written with nothing drawn to say so. The replacement counts as one cluster wherever clusters are counted. It applies to every drawn piece of text, received or sent, in the two human clients that draw (the command-line client prints the source's bytes to a terminal that is not its renderer, and stands where Copy does) — body, preview, the conversation list's line, link control labels, Show source, the plain-text fallback, the composer and the text the client hands the platform to show — and a draft's cluster is replaced in the draft, so what is sent is what was shown; Copy and the bridge carry the bytes untouched, since neither draws; so the source stays available whole, by Copy, while Show source draws it under the bounds like the body. A link whose control's label, as shown, would contain such a cluster gets no control, for the reason the label rule gives: a replaced cluster in a label hides part of where the link goes. The condition any other figure must keep is written beside it: every cluster at the figure, in each script class the shaper clusters differently, draws on both clients, and the shaper's own cluster never spans more than one extended grapheme cluster of text within the figure; a failed measurement lowers the figure or makes the shaper's cluster the counted unit. Measured on the desktop; Android binds the same and is measured on its device run. The matrix row and its tests land with rust-ui-dev's build, the code fix in the same pull request as this note.
