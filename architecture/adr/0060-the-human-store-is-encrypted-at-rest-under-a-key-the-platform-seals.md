# The human store is encrypted at rest, whole, under a per-profile key the platform's custody seals

**Status:** Accepted — the owner's direction of 2026-10-21 ("all contacts and conversations data should go in secure storage if not already"); the mechanism is selected by SPIKE-011 within the bounds of decision 4, as ADR-0038 left its envelope to SPIKE-007

## Context

The human store (STATE.md, `crates/human/store`) is one SQLite file under the profile's state root, `<state_dir>/human`, holding the contacts and their routes, the conversation index, the pending outbound and the unread and kept inbound message content, the settings and the read pairs. What protects it at rest today is what protects the desktop identity key under ADR-0038's `filesystem-only`: the store directory is created `0700` and the file `0600`, and the crate refuses to open a store whose permissions are wider (`StoreError::PermissionsTooOpen`). ADR-0044 reduced what is retained precisely because of "plaintext-at-rest/privacy exposure" and says the remaining content "need[s] normal application-store protections"; RETENTION.md requires that released content be absent from every file of the store — the write-ahead log and freed pages included — which the crate meets with SQLite's secure-delete and a checkpointing discipline. Nothing encrypts any of it: a copy of the file, a backup of the directory, or a forensic read of the device's flash after the owner-only check is bypassed, yields every name, route and retained message in clear.

Two things changed the weight of that. ADR-0059 makes contacts a thing people exchange, so the store will hold names and faces beside PeerIds for every counterparty, and the owner directed that contacts and conversation data go in secure storage. And on Android the project already has the custody the store lacks: the identity seed is sealed under an `AndroidKeyStore` AES-GCM key (ADR-0042, the IWK1 envelope in `crates/transport/embedded/src/custody.rs`), with two unlock policies, `background-compatible` and `user-presence`, measured by SPIKE-009. The desktop has no such custody in standard v1 (ADR-0038 keeps `filesystem-only` and defines a passphrase envelope for v2.x, pending SPIKE-007).

The repository already resolves `rusqlite` with the `bundled` amalgamation, and the same crate offers an amalgamation built with a page-level cipher (`bundled-sqlcipher`, with or without a vendored TLS library for the cipher primitives); `aes-gcm`, `chacha20poly1305`, `hkdf` and `zeroize` are already in the graph. The licence allow-list of `deny.toml` holds BSD-3-Clause and Apache-2.0.

## Decision

1. **The human store is encrypted at rest as a whole**: every page of the database file, its write-ahead log and its journal, so that the contact rows, the conversation index, the retained message content, the indexes over them and the freed pages are all ciphertext on disk. Encrypting content columns and leaving the structure in clear is not this record's bar (alternatives, below): a conversation index in clear is a social graph in clear.

2. **The key is a 32-byte store key, generated per profile from the system's CSPRNG, never derived from the identity seed, the recovery phrase or any transport material.** Losing it loses the store and nothing else (the store "may be deleted/rebuilt without changing PeerId or trust policy", STATE.md); it restores nothing by phrase, as contacts never did (ADR-0043, `human-client-ui.md` §9). It is held in memory only while the store is open and zeroized when it closes.

3. **The platform's custody seals the store key: on Android under the Keystore at the `background-compatible` level, on the v1 desktop at ADR-0038's `filesystem-only` level — never below the platform's floor for the identity seed, never under the seed's own key, and never derived from it.** Where the seed is held stricter (`user-presence`), the store key is deliberately not: the reason is in the Android bullet.
   - **Android**: the store key is wrapped by an `AndroidKeyStore` AES-256-GCM key in an envelope of the IWK1 construction (ADR-0042) but **under its own custody, apart from the seed's**: its own Keystore alias namespace (`interweave.store.<profile>`, never one of the seed's `interweave.iwk1.<profile>.<policy>` aliases), its own magic and associated data so a seed record and a store-key record can never authenticate in each other's place, and a key the seed's seal, reseal, restore and `retire_others` never touch — the seed custody of `crates/transport/embedded/src/custody.rs` keeps one key per (profile, policy), deletes the alias it seals under and retires the profile's other policy, so a store key inside that seam would be lost on every reseal of a `user-presence` profile and would share an alias on a `background-compatible` one. Its unlock policy is **`background-compatible`** whatever the seed's policy is: the store must open when the foreground service restarts unattended to commit unread content (STATE.md §Store health, RETENTION.md), and a store that waited for a person's presence would make every restart a loss of durability — which the identity's `user-presence` policy accepts for the identity because the identity does nothing until the person returns, and the store cannot. The seed's `user-presence` protection is not weakened by this: the store key opens the store, not the identity. Keystore invalidation of the store key is fail-closed and reported as a store-health condition, never as an identity condition, and the person is offered to discard and recreate the store; a key that is missing rather than invalidated goes through recovery mode as decision 6 orders.
   - **Desktop, standard v1**: the sealed store key is one owner-only file, `<state_dir>/human-store.key` — under the profile's state root, **outside** the human client's own directory `<state_dir>/human` so that an export or a copy of the store's directory leaves the key behind, and **not** in the daemon's identity directory, which is under the data root (`ProfilePaths::identity_dir`, `identity.key`) and is the daemon's, not the client's. That is ADR-0038's `filesystem-only` level, the protection the identity has today, and no less. When ADR-0038's v2.x passphrase envelope lands (SPIKE-007), the store key is sealed under that envelope's passphrase as well, in its own file, so one unlock opens both while the two secrets stay apart, and the desktop reaches the Android bar. What the v1 desktop gains meanwhile is stated honestly in Security implications; it is not nothing, and it is not the Android bar. **A headless custody mode stays available for an automated client's profile (A 2026-10-30)**: a profile that no person attends — an account's control profile, whose `fabric-control` endpoint a control agent holds through the command-line client (ADR-0055; agent-fabric's control plane rides it since its records of 2026-10-28) — keeps the `filesystem-only` level as a selectable custody when the passphrase envelope lands, and the envelope's own unlock has a credential source no person types and that never passes through the environment, the arguments or the IPC of the control agent that launches the client (ADR-0038 as amended), so that a restart of the daemon or of the agent that drives the client opens the store unattended, as the Android store must; a store that waited for a passphrase on such a profile would turn every restart into a loss of durability, the same reason the Android bullet gives. SPIKE-007's adoption is bounded by it: no release makes the envelope the only desktop custody.

4. **The mechanism is selected by SPIKE-011, which converts into permanent tests, within these bounds**: an authenticated page-level cipher maintained outside this repository (no bespoke construction, ADR-0038's rule); AES-256 or ChaCha20-Poly1305 class; a key file format versioned like the IWK1 envelope; built for every target the clients ship on, `aarch64-linux-android` included, from a licence already on the allow-list or one the spike's record argues for; a measured cost against a plaintext baseline the spike builds itself in the same run — no store benchmark exists in the tree today, and no document holds a store-health time budget, so SPIKE-011's first step is to define both: open, commit-unread and read-conversation timings on the plaintext store and on the encrypted one, on the desktop host and on the Stage 17 reference device, with this record's criterion that the encrypted figures stay within **twice** the plaintext baseline for each operation and that a cold open stays under one second on the device (architect-cto's bounds, to be confirmed or moved by the spike's record with its reasons). The first candidate is the page-cipher amalgamation `rusqlite` already offers, because it meets decision 1 without a schema change and keeps the deletion property of RETENTION.md inside one engine; the second is an application-level AEAD over the content columns under the same key, admitted only if the first cannot be built or exceeds that criterion, and then recorded as not meeting decision 1 in full, with the index and the conversation structure named as what stays in clear.

5. **Deletion semantics do not relax.** Encryption raises the cost of reading a freed page; it does not make released content absent. RETENTION.md's rule — secure delete, the log checkpointed, no reconstruction from the store's own files — stands unchanged, and the spike measures it on the encrypted file.

6. **A plaintext store is scrubbed while it still holds its name, and an encrypted one takes that name only afterwards, with the end of each step marked durably so no crash loses the only complete copy.** The store's one file is `human.sqlite` (`crates/human/store`, `STORE_FILE`). The first open after this lands on a profile whose `human.sqlite` is plaintext: (a) generates the store key, writes and syncs its sealed file `<state_dir>/human-store.key`, exports the store into a new encrypted file under that key at `human.sqlite.encrypting`, syncs it, renames it to `human.sqlite.encrypted` and syncs the directory — that rename is the durable mark that a complete copy exists; (b) scrubs the plaintext in place while it is still `human.sqlite` — every row deleted under `secure_delete`, the file vacuumed, the write-ahead log checkpointed and truncated — then unlinks its `-wal` and `-shm` and last the file itself, and syncs the directory; (c) renames `human.sqlite.encrypted` to `human.sqlite` and syncs the directory. Every crash state is told apart by name on the next open: a plaintext `human.sqlite` beside `human.sqlite.encrypting` means (a) was incomplete, so the temporary file is discarded and the rewrite starts again from the intact plaintext; a `human.sqlite` beside `human.sqlite.encrypted` means the complete copy exists and (b) was under way, so the encrypted file is kept and the scrub resumes on whatever the plaintext still holds; `human.sqlite.encrypted` alone means (b) completed, so (c) is done; a `human.sqlite` that opens under the key is the encrypted store. **What the next open does with a `human.sqlite` that is not readable plaintext, in this order:** a sealed key is present and the file opens under it — the store; the platform's custody reports the key invalidated (Android, where the wrapped record is present and the Keystore refuses it) — the key-invalidated condition, discard-and-recreate offered directly; otherwise — a present key that does not open the file, no key file at all (on the desktop a lost key and a never-migrated damaged plaintext leave the same files and nothing tells them apart), or any combination of names this decision does not list — the store goes to STATE.md's recovery mode, whose screen gains **Discard and recreate** as a third, confirmed action beside Quit and Retry, so no state traps the person and nothing is deleted without their word. A confirmed discard removes `human.sqlite` with its `-wal` and `-shm`; it removes the sealed key only when no `human.sqlite.encrypted` exists, because that file is the complete copy sealed under that very key — with it present the key is kept and the next open promotes the copy as (c) does, so a discard during a stalled rewrite loses the half-scrubbed plaintext and nothing else; whether the copy can be opened is found by trying — the discard opens `human.sqlite.encrypted` read-only under the key before it builds the confirmation — and when it cannot, for any reason (no key, a key the platform reports invalidated, a key that does not open it, a damaged copy), the discard removes the copy and the key as well, in one confirmed step. What this does not claim is RETENTION.md's own disclaimer: flash remanence, filesystem journals and snapshots are outside what application code can clear, and the clients say so where they explain it. Nothing has shipped, so no released build holds a plaintext store; Stage 19's upgrade and rollback rows (plan §22, rust-ui-dev's) carry the case anyway, because a rollback to a build that reads only plaintext must refuse the encrypted profile by name, not treat it as corruption — and the name is ADR-0028's profile-format marker (A 2026-10-20, `<state>/profile-format.json`), not the file's: step (a) raises the profile format before the rename, so every build since the marker refuses the profile, naming the file, both numbers and that a newer build wrote it, writing nothing; a plaintext open of the encrypted file (`NotADatabase`, the same error as a corrupt file or a wrong key) is never the path a rollback takes, and a build from before the marker can only read it as corruption, which is ADR-0028's stated limit and binds no release (A 2026-10-21, SPIKE-011's first desktop finding).

7. **What is in the store is unchanged by this record.** ADR-0044's three durable states, STATE.md's tables and the exclusion of the whole store from Android system backup stay as they are. The "future explicit encrypted application backup" of ADR-0044 is bounded by decision 8.

8. **InterWeave data never leaves the device through a platform backup, a platform transfer or any external provider; the backup that will exist travels over InterWeave itself, between the person's own devices** — the owner's words of 2026-10-21 ("interweave data must never go on backup on cloud or work with app to move between phones"; "the backup for the future will be done between the app via interweave itself eventually, not via external provider"). The first half makes permanent what `android-key-custody.md` §Android backup and device-transfer policy and STATE.md §Backup policy held for standard v1: on Android the application declares `android:allowBackup="false"` and extraction rules that exclude every domain from both `cloud-backup` and `device-transfer` (`apps/human-android/android/app/src/main/res/xml/data_extraction_rules.xml`; no test reads the manifest today — the retention conformance suite records its item 13 as a packaging property it cannot prove — so the manifest check plan §29 step 2 owes under ADR-0059 decision 6 pins these two declarations as well), and never adopts a backup agent, a platform account sync or a device-to-device transfer path; the store, its sealed key, the identity envelope and the configuration are all inside that exclusion. On desktop the client writes nothing outside the profile's state root and registers nothing with a platform sync or backup service. No cloud, vendor or third-party service of any kind ever holds InterWeave data, encrypted or not. The second half decides the shape of the backup ADR-0044 left open: when it comes, it is an application feature between two of the person's own devices — two PeerIds (ADR-0043), each with its own identity — carried over InterWeave's own direct protocol like any application payload, end to end between the two clients, under a key the two devices establish for it, with ADR-0044's bounds on what message content may travel; designed in its own record, with its own threat model, pairing, consent and replay rules, and not by this one. Until that record exists there is no backup and no move: a new device starts from the recovery phrase, for the identity alone (ADR-0033, ADR-0043), and an empty store.

## Alternatives considered

- **Owner-only file permissions, as today.** Rejected on the owner's direction and on ADR-0044's own words: the retained content is sensitive at rest and the file is in clear to any copy, backup or same-account process; on Android a rooted device or a flash image reads it whole.
- **Encrypt content columns only** (message payloads, names, photos) and keep the schema in clear. Rejected as the design, admitted as the fallback (decision 4): the conversation index, the routes and the timestamps are the social graph, and indexes over encrypted columns either do not work or leak order.
- **Derive the store key from the identity seed.** Rejected: it binds the store's secrecy to the seed's and makes the recovery phrase a key to every past store; the seed is the identity, the store is replaceable.
- **`user-presence` for the store key on Android.** Rejected: unread durability is the human client's property (STATE.md §Store health) and the service commits unread content while the person is away; a store that cannot open then loses what the retention contract promises to keep. The seed's stricter policy stays its own.
- **A passphrase for the store on desktop now.** Rejected for v1 as ADR-0038 rejected it for the identity: an unlock path and a second secret, before SPIKE-007 has chosen the envelope that both will use. The store joins that envelope when it lands.
- **A bespoke AEAD-over-pages layer in the store crate.** Rejected: ADR-0038's rule against inventing cipher formats applies with more force to a database file, whose page structure, write-ahead log and crash recovery are exactly where a home-made layer fails.
- **Let the platform's backup carry the store now that it is encrypted.** Rejected on the owner's word: an encrypted copy on a cloud account, or moved by the platform to a second phone, is still a copy the person did not make, held by a provider, under a key that would then have to travel with it; the exclusion stays total and the one future copy is the one InterWeave's own protocol carries between the person's devices.
- **Encrypt only once a SPIKE has chosen, with nothing decided now.** Rejected: the property is the decision and is the owner's; the mechanism is what a spike selects, as ADR-0038 did.

## Consequences

The store gains a key, a sealed key file, a migration and a spike; the clients gain one more store-health condition (key unavailable or invalidated) with its recovery action. Opening the store on Android now goes through the Keystore once per process, which SPIKE-009 measured for the seed and costs the same here. Benchmarks of the store move by whatever SPIKE-011 measures, within the budget. The dependency graph changes by the chosen amalgamation and, if the cipher primitives need it on the Android target, a vendored TLS library — a `deny.toml` licence entry and a `check_vendored_advisories`-class concern the spike's record names before the PR that adds it.

## Security implications

**What this protects against**: a copy of the profile directory or the device's storage taken without the sealed key — a backup, a stolen disk image, a forensic read of Android flash, a same-account process that reads files but cannot reach the Keystore. On Android the store key is as hard to extract as the seed under `background-compatible`: hardware-backed where the device has it, software Keystore where not, reported in diagnostics as ADR-0042 already does, and an attacker with code execution inside the app's process can open the store exactly as they could unwrap the seed. **What it does not protect against**: an unlocked device with the app running, a process with the app's uid on Android, or on the v1 desktop a same-user process or anyone holding the whole state root — there the sealed key file `<state_dir>/human-store.key` sits one directory above the store at the same `filesystem-only` level, so the gain is against a copy of the store's directory or file alone (a crash dump, a misdirected backup or export of `<state_dir>/human` and not the key, a tool that reads SQLite files) and against the clear-text read of freed pages, until ADR-0038's envelope moves both secrets behind a passphrase. This record does not change what is retained (ADR-0044), who can send what (ADR-0012), or the threat model of a same-uid process (ADR-0037, the plan's §28). Key material never enters logs, diagnostics, messages, the endpoint directory or IPC; a diagnostic names the protection level and the key's presence, never a byte of it.

## Operational implications

A store that cannot open because its key is invalidated, or that reaches recovery mode because its key is missing or the file is damaged (decision 6's order), has one way back to a working store, discard and recreate — offered directly for an invalidated key, as the recovery screen's confirmed third action otherwise, and as a command of the command-line client (ADR-0059 decision 9), which reports the store's health and the key's presence the same way and performs the discard only with an explicit confirmation flag, refusing without it, since nothing is deleted without the person's word — which loses everything in the store on that device (STATE.md §Logical tables): every contact and its routes, pending outbound, unread and kept content, the conversation list, the duplicate-suppression pairs (so a message delivered again shows again), the client's settings and the person's own cards — and no identity, no trust policy and no transport configuration, none of which the store holds; the clients list exactly that before doing it. The one exception is a discard with `human.sqlite.encrypted` present: it removes the unfinished plaintext alone and the screen says so, since the next open promotes the complete copy; and when that copy cannot be opened either — tried read-only under the key before the confirmation is built, so a damaged copy or a key that does not open it count as much as a missing or invalidated key — the same confirmed discard removes the copy and the key with the plaintext, so the loss is listed once and taken once, never at a second screen. Android cloud backup and device transfer never carry the store, its sealed key or anything else of the application (decision 8); the Keystore key was never transferable, so the exclusion that was policy is now also fact. Until the InterWeave-carried backup of decision 8 has its own record, a new phone starts from the phrase and an empty store, and the clients say so where they explain recovery (`human-client-ui.md` §9). A desktop profile moved between machines by copying its whole state root keeps working in v1, since the sealed key file moves with it, while a copy of `<state_dir>/human` alone opens nothing; after SPIKE-007 it asks for the passphrase. Stage 19's upgrade and rollback rows gain the plaintext-to-encrypted case and its refusal on rollback. On an automated client's profile (A 2026-10-30) the destructive command-line operations are never the control agent's: `store discard --confirm <scope>` is the operator's, run by a person over the host's administrative path (the deployment's, not the carrier's); `identity new-key --confirm key-change`, the client's own verb for a lost key until the release that builds ADR-0067, is the operator's the same way, and from that release the way out of a lost endpoint key is the one ADR-0067 decision 5 gives every endpoint — a new identity, which for a rendered profile is `transportctl endpoint init` and a re-render of the roster by the deployment, on the owner's word, never a key under the old name. The control agent drives `listen` and `send` alone; that today's client makes an endpoint's key on its first run (the silent first key) is a minting those verbs still carry until ADR-0067's release withdraws it, after which a rendered profile's keys are the deployment's and no verb the agent drives mints or deletes.

## Implementation implications

SPIKE-011 (the roadmap's SPIKES.md, plan §3's table) runs before the store work of plan §29 and before any Stage 19 release that ships a store: it builds the first candidate for the Linux and Android targets, defines the plaintext baseline and measures open, commit and read on it and on the encrypted store against decision 4's criterion, measures RETENTION.md's deletion property on the encrypted file, names the licence and the vendored-advisory consequences, and its Result either pins the candidate or records why the fallback is taken. The crate work is rust-ui-dev's: the key file and its versioned format, the Android sealing in its own alias namespace apart from the seed custody (whose holder is named in the remits), the desktop key file at `<state_dir>/human-store.key`, the migration of decision 6, the health condition and its action, and the conformance cases plan §29 names. STATE.md §Selected store carries one sentence citing this record; `android-key-custody.md` §Selected v1 Android design gains the second sealed secret and its policy when the Android half lands.

## Revisit conditions

When SPIKE-007 lands ADR-0038's envelope (the desktop store key moves into it); when the InterWeave-carried backup between the person's devices (decision 8) is designed, in its own record; when SPIKE-011's measurement exceeds decision 4's criterion on a shipping device class; when a desktop platform offers a custody the project decides to use (an OS keychain, ADR-0038's rejected-for-now alternative); or when the Android unlock policies of ADR-0042 change.
