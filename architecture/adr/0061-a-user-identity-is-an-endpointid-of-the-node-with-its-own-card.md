# A user identity is an EndpointId of the node with its own card: a person holds several under one PeerId, and every conversation speaks as exactly one

**Status:** Accepted — the owner's direction of 2026-10-21 ("a user should have multiple network identities"), clarified the same day on the record's first draft: "An EndpointId should match a user identity. One user of the app can have many user identities on the same phone. So for any EndpointId he can add a display_name and a photo", and the shape below — one PeerId per node, the identities its endpoints, the profile per identity kept as the heavier separation for the case that needs it — agreed in the owner's words ("I agree, write in that way"), with the rule added the same hour: "two endpointid are two identities even if they belong to one user. Avoid any ux that can let the user think that is operating with one while operating instead with the other"

## Context

Two identifiers exist and the records keep them apart. The **PeerId** is the node's cryptographic identity: one per profile (ADR-0016), one per concurrently active device (ADR-0043), the hash of the key the 24-word phrase restores (ADR-0033); it is what a connection authenticates, what a broadcast is signed with, what trust is granted to and what routing finds. The **EndpointId** is a configured routing selector under it (ADR-0030, `ENDPOINTS.md`): at most 64 per profile, registered only by configuration, each held by at most one direct-capable local session through an exclusive lease, never a second PeerId, never an authorization principal; on the wire a remote `source_endpoint` is peer-asserted metadata beside the authenticated `source_peer`. The human clients enable one endpoint, `human` (`human-client-ui.md` §2 step 4), and ADR-0059 let a person keep several cards of their own under that one PeerId as presentation.

The owner's direction is that a person holds several identities and chooses which one speaks. The first draft of this record read "identity" as a whole profile — its own key, PeerId, phrase, daemon and store — and the owner corrected it: the user identity is the EndpointId, each with its display name and photo, many on one phone. What follows is that model, with what it does not give stated beside it.

## Decision

1. **A user identity is a configured EndpointId of the profile together with the card the person gives it**: a display name — a name and an optional surname — and an optional photo, the card of ADR-0059. The node stays one: one PeerId and key, one recovery phrase, one trust set, one human store, one custody, one daemon or embedded runtime, one set of listeners and relay reservations, shared by every identity it holds. On the wire nothing changes: the EndpointId remains a routing selector that grants no trust and is authenticated only by the PeerId it sits under (ADR-0030 A 2026-10-21, ADR-0032); the counterparty learns which identity spoke from the `source_endpoint` the direct protocol already carries. ADR-0059's own cards become **one card per identity**, the identity's presentation (ADR-0059 A 2026-10-21 (ii)).

2. **Creating an identity is configuring an endpoint.** Registration stays configured-only (`ENDPOINTS.md`): a data-plane session cannot mint an endpoint by naming one. The client creates, renames and removes an identity through the administrative boundary — on the desktop over the admin connection, on Android through the embedded host's configuration path, the same seam that writes the stay-reachable choice — and enabling, disabling and the default live in ADR-0028's endpoint overlay (A 2026-10-11). The 64-endpoint bound caps the identities a node holds. The administrative method that adds a configured endpoint is p2p-network-dev's to shape, under ADR-0017's minor rule, and what it adds persists in ADR-0028's endpoint overlay — amended with this record (A 2026-10-21) to carry an added endpoint's definition beside the enabled and default deltas, so that `config.yaml` stays authored and unrewritten and the 64 bound counts both; until the method lands, a desktop identity is added by editing the profile's configuration, and on Android, where the profile is app-private and `runtime.android.endpoint` is one field, no second identity exists. Each identity the client presents holds **its own endpoint lease**: one local data session per identity — one IPC connection each on the desktop, one in-process session each on Android — inside the one runtime, so a client holding N identities holds N leases and the lease rules of `ENDPOINTS.md` hold unchanged.

3. **A conversation speaks as exactly one identity.** A direct conversation is keyed `{local EndpointId, remote PeerId, remote EndpointId?}`; everything it sends — messages, cards, receipts — leaves from that identity's lease, so the counterparty's `source_endpoint` names it, and it never changes identity. A channel conversation belongs to one identity too, but broadcast authorship is PeerId-only (ADR-0030): the identity travels as the `from_endpoint` display hint HUMAN-CHAT.md defines, unauthenticated beyond the PeerId, and the record promises nothing more for it. Whenever the client holds more than one identity, starting a conversation asks which one speaks, and every conversation shows it in its details and beside the composer; a client holding one identity asks nothing and shows the identity only where it is asked for. Two identities of one person are two identities to the client as much as to the counterparty (the owner's rule): nothing the person does under one is done under the other, and no surface may let them act as one while believing they act as the other — decision 9 says how. The profile's default endpoint (ADR-0030) is the identity a peer-only send reaches, and the person chooses it among their identities.

4. **A contact is one remote identity, held by the local identity that accepted it**: a route `{PeerId, EndpointId}` with the name and photo of the card accepted on it, under the local EndpointId whose conversation the card arrived in; the same counterparty accepted by two of the person's identities is two contacts, one per identity, and neither identity sees the other's. Two endpoints of one PeerId are two identities and two contacts; the contact page shows the PeerId and says which other contacts share that node, and the client never merges them on its own and never splits one route in two. Routes of one person on other devices — other PeerIds, ADR-0043 — may be grouped under one contact by the person, never by the client, and a contact holds at most one route per PeerId. Accept on a route already under a contact updates that contact; Accept on a new route creates one; Move moves the one route. This replaces the rule of the same morning that a PeerId never ends under two contacts (ADR-0059 A 2026-10-21 (ii) withdraws it).

5. **What two identities of one node share is said, not hidden.** They share the PeerId and the node's addresses, so a counterparty who holds two of them sees one node — the same PeerId beside both contacts — and the network, a relay both use and the LAN see one device. The identity settings and the contact page say so in plain words. The separation for a person who needs two identities that no counterparty can link is **a second profile**: its own PeerId, phrase, daemon and store, which the desktop runs today as a second daemon and a second client, and the phone cannot (one embedded runtime, ADR-0041); even then both profiles leave from the host's addresses, so the network-level limit stays. The record names that form for that case and makes it no stage of its own.

6. **Recovery restores the node, not the identities.** The 24-word phrase restores the key and PeerId (ADR-0033) and ADR-0043's restore warning applies to the node. Identities are configuration — the endpoint list and its overlay — and store content — their cards: carried by the desktop's configuration export, by nothing on Android today, and by the device-to-device backup of ADR-0060 decision 8 only if its record says so.

7. **Every action has a command-line form** (ADR-0059 decision 9): list the identities the client holds, create one, rename it — the display name on its card, never the EndpointId, which keys its conversations and contacts for life — set its photo, set the default, remove one — an identity with conversations only on an explicit confirmed action — and name the identity on every conversation and contact command.

8. **Automated clients are unchanged**: an agent's client holds the endpoint ADR-0055 shapes for it, which is a route and not a user identity and carries no card.

9. **The client operates as one identity at a time, and says which, everywhere.** The one store carries the local EndpointId on every conversation (`conversation_index`), every contact (`contacts`) and every own card (`own_cards`, STATE.md), and the client shows only the identity in front: its conversations, its contacts, its card, its default. Switching identity is one explicit act — a control that names the identity in front on every screen that can send, share, accept, create or delete, and the composer names it beside the send control — and the switch changes everything shown at once; there is no mixed list, no search across identities, no suggestion from one identity's contacts in another's chooser, and no action whose identity is implied by state the person cannot see. A notification names the identity it arrived on and opening it switches to that identity visibly before anything is shown; a share, an Accept and a new conversation run under the identity in front and nothing else. The Android service's one persistent notification names the node, since there is one runtime. The command-line client names the identity on every command that acts (decision 7) and defaults to none.

## Alternatives considered

- **An identity is a whole profile** (this record's first draft). Rejected as the everyday form: a phone would run one runtime per identity — listeners, relay reservations, Kademlia membership, custody and a foreground service each — for a separation the network does not give, since every profile on the host shares its addresses. Kept as the heavier form of decision 5 for the person who needs unlinkability against a counterparty.
- **Several personas under one endpoint** (ADR-0059's cards as they stood). Rejected: a counterparty cannot address one persona, a conversation's identity would be a client-side flag the wire does not carry, and a reply would reach whichever persona the person had in front.
- **Hidden sub-keys under one PeerId.** Rejected by ADR-0016 and ADR-0043, and nothing here needs them.
- **Per-identity signatures above transport**, so that a broadcast's `from_endpoint` is authenticated. Deferred: ADR-0030's revisit condition, with no consumer asking yet.
- **A second installation of the app per identity.** Rejected as the design; it stays a person's workaround.

## Consequences

ADR-0030 is amended with this record (a use of the label above transport, nothing changed in what the transport authenticates); ADR-0059 is amended a second time the same day (one card per identity, a contact per remote identity, the one-contact-per-PeerId rule withdrawn); ADR-0041 is amended (the service holds one lease per identity in its one runtime). `human-client-ui.md` §2 step 4, §3, §4 and §13, STATE.md and CLAUDE.md §5 carry the inherited sentences; plan §30 names the work as Stage 22, opened on the owner's word; SPIKE-012, which measured two runtimes in one service for the first draft, is withdrawn. The transport, the wire and every contract under `architecture/contracts/` are unchanged except `ENDPOINTS.md`'s registration sentence gaining the administrative path when p2p-network-dev lands its method, with ADR-0028's overlay amended to hold what it adds.

## Security implications

Nothing changes on the wire, so nothing weakens there: trust, admission and authorship stay the PeerId's. The risks are the client's. A send under the wrong identity — or any act under one identity the person believes they take under another, which the owner's rule forbids by name — is answered by binding the conversation to one identity for life, by the client operating as one identity at a time with that identity named on every acting surface, and by the explicit, visible switch (decisions 3 and 9). A person misled about who a contact is — two contacts shown as two people when they are one node — is answered by the contact page showing the PeerId and the shared-node note (decisions 4 and 5); the reverse, two same-name contacts on different PeerIds, stays told apart by the short PeerId (`human-client-ui.md` §13). An impersonation of one of a person's identities by another endpoint of the same node is the same person and no attack. The broadcast `from_endpoint` is a hint a counterparty may forge within its own PeerId, as HUMAN-CHAT.md already says, and the client renders it as such. The 64-endpoint bound and the lease rules keep the per-identity cost bounded; an attacker holding the device sees every identity, as every file.

## Operational implications

One runtime, one notification, one phrase: a phone with several identities costs what a phone with one costs, plus a lease and a queue per identity. An operator adds an identity as an endpoint is added today, until the administrative method lands. The default endpoint is a choice the person sees as "which identity a stranger reaches".

## Implementation implications

The work is plan §30's (Stage 22), rust-ui-dev's in the shared crates and the three clients, with the administrative add-endpoint method p2p-network-dev's; `human-client-ui.md` §13 gains the acceptance criteria when the stage opens. STATE.md's `conversation_index` and `contacts` gain the local EndpointId — existing rows backfilled with the `human` endpoint, the one identity a client holds before this record — and `own_cards` is keyed by it in the §Logical tables review; the contact rows carry the route as they do. No spike is needed: every piece — several leases in one runtime, a session per lease, `source_endpoint` on every direct message — exists and is tested.

## Revisit conditions

When per-endpoint authorship above transport is wanted (ADR-0030's revisit); when a person asks for identities on the phone that no counterparty can link, which is a second runtime in the one service — the measurement the withdrawn SPIKE-012 described; when a backup should carry identities (ADR-0060 decision 8); when the 64-endpoint bound binds.
