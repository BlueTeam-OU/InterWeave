## when a wording pass splits a 'which' clause into its own sentence, join the parts with 'and', not 'so'

When a wording pass splits a relative clause off into its own sentence, join its parts with "and". Do not join them with "so", "because" or "therefore".

**Why:** on InterWeave #642 (j298, 48d730e2d, 2026-11-12), the author wrote "the moment the reads start, which is after the seal and the service start, never before a grant". My first draft said "The reads start after the seal and the service start, so a hold never counts from before a grant." That made "never before a grant" follow from the first claim, which the author had not written. Before committing, I changed it to "The reads start after the seal and the service start, and never before a grant."

**How to apply:** check each connective a split adds against the source sentence. A causal word is a new claim about what the text means, so it goes back to the author as a question. Related: [[quote-a-condition-verbatim]], [[keep-else-whole]], [[qualifier-is-a-claim]].
